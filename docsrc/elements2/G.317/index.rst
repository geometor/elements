G.317
=====

.. rubric:: `X.4 <https://geometor.github.io/euclid/heath/volume_III/book_x/propositions/004/>`_

.. enunciation::

   Given three commensurable magnitudes, to find their greatest common measure.

Let :g:`A <0026.g1:A>`, :g:`B <0026.g1:B>`, :g:`C <0026.g1:C>` be the three given commensurable magnitudes ; thus it is required to find the greatest common measure of :g:`A <0026.g1:A>`, :g:`B <0026.g1:B>`, :g:`C <0026.g1:C>`.

.. picture:: 0026.g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 4 diagram

Let the greatest common measure of the two magnitudes :g:`A <0026.g1:A>`, :g:`B <0026.g1:B>` be taken, and let it be :g:`D <0026.g1:D>` ; :just:`[G.316] <G.316>` then :g:`D <0026.g1:D>` either measures :g:`C <0026.g1:C>`, or does not measure it.

First, let it measure it.

.. subclause::

   Since then :g:`D <0026.g1:D>` measures :g:`C <0026.g1:C>`, while it also measures :g:`A <0026.g1:A>`, :g:`B <0026.g1:B>`,

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`D <0026.g1:D>` is a common measure of :g:`A <0026.g1:A>`, :g:`B <0026.g1:B>`, :g:`C <0026.g1:C>`.

.. assertion::

   And it is manifest that it is also the greatest ; for a greater magnitude than the magnitude :g:`D <0026.g1:D>` does not measure :g:`A <0026.g1:A>`, :g:`B <0026.g1:B>`.

Next, let :g:`D <0026.g1:D>` not measure :g:`C <0026.g1:C>`.

I say first that :g:`C <0026.g1:C>`, :g:`D <0026.g1:D>` are commensurable.

.. subclause::

   For, since :g:`A <0026.g1:A>`, :g:`B <0026.g1:B>`, :g:`C <0026.g1:C>` are commensurable, some magnitude will measure them, and this will of course measure :g:`A <0026.g1:A>`, :g:`B <0026.g1:B>` also ;

.. assertion::

   so that it will also measure the greatest common measure of :g:`A <0026.g1:A>`, :g:`B <0026.g1:B>`, namely :g:`D <0026.g1:D>`. :just:`[G.316] <G.316>`

.. subclause::

   But it also measures :g:`C <0026.g1:C>` ;

.. assertion::

   so that the said magnitude will measure :g:`C <0026.g1:C>`, :g:`D <0026.g1:D>` ; therefore :g:`C <0026.g1:C>`, :g:`D <0026.g1:D>` are commensurable.

Now let their greatest common measure be taken, and let it be :g:`E <0026.g1:E>`. :just:`[G.316] <G.316>`

.. subclause::

   Since then :g:`E <0026.g1:E>` measures :g:`D <0026.g1:D>`, while :g:`D <0026.g1:D>` measures :g:`A <0026.g1:A>`, :g:`B <0026.g1:B>`,

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`E <0026.g1:E>` will also measure :g:`A <0026.g1:A>`, :g:`B <0026.g1:B>`.

.. subclause::

   But it measures :g:`C <0026.g1:C>` also ;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`E <0026.g1:E>` measures :g:`A <0026.g1:A>`, :g:`B <0026.g1:B>`, :g:`C <0026.g1:C>` ; therefore :g:`E <0026.g1:E>` is a common measure of :g:`A <0026.g1:A>`, :g:`B <0026.g1:B>`, :g:`C <0026.g1:C>`.

I say next that it is also the greatest.

For, if possible, let there be some magnitude :g:`F <0026.g1:F>` greater than :g:`E <0026.g1:E>`, and let it measure :g:`A <0026.g1:A>`, :g:`B <0026.g1:B>`, :g:`C <0026.g1:C>`.

.. subclause::

   Now, since :g:`F <0026.g1:F>` measures :g:`A <0026.g1:A>`, :g:`B <0026.g1:B>`, :g:`C <0026.g1:C>`, it will also measure :g:`A <0026.g1:A>`, :g:`B <0026.g1:B>`, and will measure the greatest common measure of :g:`A <0026.g1:A>`, :g:`B <0026.g1:B>`. :just:`[G.316] <G.316>`

.. subclause::

   But the greatest common measure of :g:`A <0026.g1:A>`, :g:`B <0026.g1:B>` is :g:`D <0026.g1:D>` ;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`F <0026.g1:F>` measures :g:`D <0026.g1:D>`.

.. subclause::

   But it measures :g:`C <0026.g1:C>` also ;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`F <0026.g1:F>` measures :g:`C <0026.g1:C>`, :g:`D <0026.g1:D>` ; therefore :g:`F <0026.g1:F>` will also measure the greatest common measure of :g:`C <0026.g1:C>`, :g:`D <0026.g1:D>`. :just:`[G.316] <G.316>`

.. subclause::

   But that is :g:`E <0026.g1:E>` ;

.. assertion::

   therefore :g:`F <0026.g1:F>` will measure :g:`E <0026.g1:E>`, the greater the less : which is impossible.

.. assertion::

   Therefore no magnitude greater than the magnitude :g:`E <0026.g1:E>` will measure :g:`A <0026.g1:A>`, :g:`B <0026.g1:B>`, :g:`C <0026.g1:C>` ; therefore :g:`E <0026.g1:E>` is the greatest common measure of :g:`A <0026.g1:A>`, :g:`B <0026.g1:B>`, :g:`C <0026.g1:C>` if :g:`D <0026.g1:D>` do not measure :g:`C <0026.g1:C>`, and, if it measure it, :g:`D <0026.g1:D>` is itself the greatest common measure.

.. assertion::

   Therefore the greatest common measure of the three given commensurable magnitudes has been found.

.. assertion::

   PORISM. From this it is manifest that, if a magnitude measure three magnitudes, it will also measure their greatest common measure.

.. qed::

   Similarly too, with more magnitudes, the greatest common measure can be found, and the porism can be extended. :just:`Q. E. D.`
