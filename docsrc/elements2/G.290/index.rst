G.290
=====

.. rubric:: `IX.19 <https://geometor.github.io/euclid/heath/volume_II/book_ix/propositions/19/>`_

.. enunciation::

   Given three numbers, to investigate when it is possible to find a fourth proportional to them.

.. picture:: g1.svg
   :alt: Proposition 19

.. step:: given

   :g:`A <g1:A>`, :g:`B <g1:B>`, :g:`C <g1:C>` be the given three numbers

.. step:: assert

   Let it be required to investigate when it is possible to find a fourth proportional to them.

.. step:: assert

   Now either they are not in continued proportion, and the extremes of them are prime to one another

.. step:: assert

   Or they are in continued proportion, and the extremes of them are not prime to one another

.. step:: assert

   Or they are not in continued proportion, nor are the extremes of them prime to one another

.. step:: assert

   Or they are in continued proportion, and the extremes of them are prime to one another.

.. step:: assert

   If then :g:`A <g1:A>`, :g:`B <g1:B>`, :g:`C <g1:C>` are in continued proportion, and the extremes of them :g:`A <g1:A>`, :g:`C <g1:C>` are prime to one another,

.. step:: assert [G.288] <G.288>

   It has been proved that it is impossible to find a fourth proportional number to them.

.. step:: assert

   †Next, let :g:`A <g1:A>`, :g:`B <g1:B>`, :g:`C <g1:C>` not be in continued proportion, the extremes being again prime to one another

.. step:: assert

   If possible, let :g:`D <g1:D>` have been found, so that, as :g:`A <g1:A>` is to :g:`B <g1:B>`, so is :g:`C <g1:C>` to :g:`D <g1:D>`

.. step:: assert

   Let it be contrived that, as :g:`B <g1:B>` is to :g:`C <g1:C>`, so is :g:`D <g1:D>` to :g:`E <g1:E>`.

.. step:: assert

   Since, as :g:`A <g1:A>` is to :g:`B <g1:B>`, so is :g:`C <g1:C>` to :g:`D <g1:D>`, and, as :g:`B <g1:B>` is to :g:`C <g1:C>`, so is :g:`D <g1:D>` to :g:`E <g1:E>`,

.. step:: assert [G.214] <G.214>

   Therefore, :term:`*ex aequali* <ex-aequali>`, as :g:`A <g1:A>` is to :g:`C <g1:C>`, so is :g:`C <g1:C>` to :g:`E <g1:E>`.

.. step:: assert [G.221, G.220] <G.221, G.220>

   :g:`A <g1:A>`, :g:`C <g1:C>` are prime, primes are also least, and the least numbers measure those which have the same ratio, the antecedent the antecedent and the consequent the consequent.

.. step:: assert

   Therefore :g:`A <g1:A>` measures :g:`C <g1:C>` as antecedent antecedent.

.. step:: assert

   It also measures itself ;

.. step:: assert

   Therefore :g:`A <g1:A>` measures :g:`A <g1:A>`, :g:`C <g1:C>` which are prime to one another : which is impossible.

.. step:: assert

   Therefore it is not possible to find a fourth proportional to :g:`A <g1:A>`, :g:`B <g1:B>`, :g:`C <g1:C>`.†

.. step:: assert

   Next, let :g:`A <g1:A>`, :g:`B <g1:B>`, :g:`C <g1:C>` be again in continued proportion, but let :g:`A <g1:A>`, :g:`C <g1:C>` not be prime to one another.

.. step:: assert

   For let :g:`B <g1:B>` by multiplying :g:`C <g1:C>` make :g:`D <g1:D>`

.. step:: assert

   Therefore :g:`A <g1:A>` either measures :g:`D <g1:D>` or does not measure it.

.. step:: assert

   First, let it measure it according to :g:`E <g1:E>`

.. step:: assert

   Therefore :g:`A <g1:A>` by multiplying :g:`E <g1:E>` has made :g:`D <g1:D>`.

.. step:: assert

   But, further, :g:`B <g1:B>` has also by multiplying :g:`C <g1:C>` made :g:`D <g1:D>` ;

.. step:: assert [G.219] <G.219>

   Therefore the product of :g:`A <g1:A>`, :g:`E <g1:E>` is equal to the product of :g:`B <g1:B>`, :g:`C <g1:C>` ; therefore, proportionally, as :g:`A <g1:A>` is to :g:`B <g1:B>`, so is :g:`C <g1:C>` to :g:`E <g1:E>` ; therefore :g:`E <g1:E>` has been found a fourth proportional to :g:`A <g1:A>`, :g:`B <g1:B>`, :g:`C <g1:C>`.

.. step:: assert

   Next, let :g:`A <g1:A>` not measure :g:`D <g1:D>`

.. step:: assert

   If possible, let :g:`E <g1:E>` have been found

.. step:: assert [G.219] <G.219>

   Therefore the product of :g:`A <g1:A>`, :g:`E <g1:E>` is equal to the product of :g:`B <g1:B>`, :g:`C <g1:C>`.

.. step:: assert

   The product of :g:`B <g1:B>`, :g:`C <g1:C>` is :g:`D <g1:D>` ;

.. step:: assert

   Therefore the product of :g:`A <g1:A>`, :g:`E <g1:E>` is also equal to :g:`D <g1:D>`.

.. step:: assert

   Therefore :g:`A <g1:A>` by multiplying :g:`E <g1:E>` has made :g:`D <g1:D>` ; therefore :g:`A <g1:A>` measures :g:`D <g1:D>` according to :g:`E <g1:E>`, so that :g:`A <g1:A>` measures :g:`D <g1:D>`.

.. step:: assert

   It also does not measure it :

.. step:: assert

   Which is absurd.

.. step:: assert

   Therefore it is not possible to find a fourth proportional number to :g:`A <g1:A>`, :g:`B <g1:B>`, :g:`C <g1:C>` when :g:`A <g1:A>` does not measure :g:`D <g1:D>`.

.. step:: assert

   Next, let :g:`A <g1:A>`, :g:`B <g1:B>`, :g:`C <g1:C>` not be in continued proportion, nor the extremes prime to one another.

.. step:: assert

   Let :g:`B <g1:B>` by multiplying :g:`C <g1:C>` make :g:`D <g1:D>`.

.. step:: conclude

   Similarly then it can be proved that, if :g:`A <g1:A>` measures :g:`D <g1:D>`, it is possible to find a fourth proportional to them, but, if it does not measure it, impossible.
