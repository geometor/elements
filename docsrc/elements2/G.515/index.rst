G.515
=====

.. rubric:: `XIII.8 <https://geometor.github.io/euclid/heath/volume_III/book_xiii/propositions/08/>`_

.. enunciation::

   If in an equilateral and equiangular pentagon straight lines subtend two angles taken in order, they cut one another in extreme and mean ratio, and their greater segments are equal to the side of the pentagon.

For in the :eq:`equilateral <0457.g1:/ A B / = 0457.g1:/ B C / = 0457.g1:/ C D / = 0457.g1:/ D E / = 0457.g1:/ E A />` and equiangular pentagon :g:`< A B C D E > <0457.g1:< A B C D E >>` let the straight lines :g:`/ A C / <0457.g1:/ A C />`, :g:`/ B E / <0457.g1:/ B E />`, cutting one another at the point :g:`H <0457.g1:H>`, subtend two angles taken in order, the angles at :g:`A <0457.g1:A>`, :g:`B <0457.g1:B>`; I say that each of them has been cut in extreme and mean ratio at the point :g:`H <0457.g1:H>`, and their greater segments are equal to the side of the pentagon.

.. picture:: 0457.g1.svg
   :alt: Book XIII, Proposition 8

For let the circle :g:`( A B C D E ) <0457.g1:( A B C D E )>` be circumscribed about the pentagon :g:`< A B C D E > <0457.g1:< A B C D E >>`. :just:`[G.132] <G.132>`

.. subclause::

   Then, since the two straight lines :g:`/ E A / <0457.g1:/ E A />`, :g:`/ A B / <0457.g1:/ A B />` are :eq:`equal <0457.g1:/ E A / = 0457.g1:/ A B / = 0457.g1:/ B C />` to the two :g:`/ A B / <0457.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`/ B C / <0457.g1:/ B C />`, and they contain equal angles,

.. assertion::

   therefore the base :g:`/ B E / <0457.g1:/ B E />` is :eq:`equal <0457.g1:/ B E / = 0457.g1:/ A C />` to the base :g:`/ A C / <0457.g1:/ A C />`, the triangle :g:`< A B E > <0457.g1:< A B E >>` is :eq:`equal <0457.g1:< A B E > = 0457.g1:< A B C >>` to the triangle :g:`< A B C > <0457.g1:< A B C >>`, and the remaining angles will be equal to the remaining angles respectively, namely those which the equal sides subtend. :just:`[G.11] <G.11>`

.. assertion::

   Therefore the angle :g:`< B A C ) <0457.g1:< B A C )>` is :eq:`equal <0457.g1:< B A C ) = 0457.g1:< A B E )>` to the angle :g:`< A B E ) <0457.g1:< A B E )>`; therefore the angle :g:`< A H E ) <0457.g1:< A H E )>` is double of the angle :g:`< B A H ) <0457.g1:< B A H )>`. :just:`[G.45] <G.45>`

.. subclause::

   But the angle :g:`< E A C ) <0457.g1:< E A C )>` is also double of the angle :g:`< B A C ) <0457.g1:< B A C )>`, inasmuch as the circumference :g:`( E D C ) <0457.g1:( E D C )>` is also double of the circumference :g:`( C B ) <0457.g1:( C B )>`; :just:`[G.107, G.198] <G.107, G.198>`

.. assertion::

   therefore the angle :g:`< H A E ) <0457.g1:< H A E )>` is :eq:`equal <0457.g1:< H A E ) = 0457.g1:< A H E )>` to the angle :g:`< A H E ) <0457.g1:< A H E )>`; hence the straight line :g:`/ H E / <0457.g1:/ H E />` is also :eq:`equal <0457.g1:/ H E / = 0457.g1:/ E A / = 0457.g1:/ A B />` to :g:`/ E A / <0457.g1:/ E A />`, that is, to :g:`/ A B / <0457.g1:/ A B />`. :just:`[G.13] <G.13>`

.. subclause::

   And, since the straight line :g:`/ B A / <0457.g1:/ B A />` is :eq:`equal <0457.g1:/ B A / = 0457.g1:/ A E />` to :g:`/ A E / <0457.g1:/ A E />`, the angle :g:`< A B E ) <0457.g1:< A B E )>` is also :eq:`equal <0457.g1:< A B E ) = 0457.g1:< A E B )>` to the angle :g:`< A E B ) <0457.g1:< A E B )>`. :just:`[G.12] <G.12>`

.. subclause::

   But the angle :g:`< A B E ) <0457.g1:< A B E )>` was proved :eq:`equal <0457.g1:< A B E ) = 0457.g1:< B A H )>` to the angle :g:`< B A H ) <0457.g1:< B A H )>`;

.. assertion::

   therefore the angle :g:`< B E A ) <0457.g1:< B E A )>` is also :eq:`equal <0457.g1:< B E A ) = 0457.g1:< B A H )>` to the angle :g:`< B A H ) <0457.g1:< B A H )>`.

.. subclause::

   And the angle :g:`< A B E ) <0457.g1:< A B E )>` is common to the two triangles :g:`< A B E > <0457.g1:< A B E >>` and :g:`< A B H > <0457.g1:< A B H >>`;

.. assertion::

   therefore the remaining angle :g:`< B A E ) <0457.g1:< B A E )>` is :eq:`equal <0457.g1:< B A E ) = 0457.g1:< A H B )>` to the remaining angle :g:`< A H B ) <0457.g1:< A H B )>`; :just:`[G.45] <G.45>` therefore the triangle :g:`< A B E > <0457.g1:< A B E >>` is equiangular with the triangle :g:`< A B H > <0457.g1:< A B H >>`; therefore, proportionally, as :g:`/ E B / <0457.g1:/ E B />` is to :g:`/ B A / <0457.g1:/ B A />`, so is :g:`/ A B / <0457.g1:/ A B />` to :g:`/ B H / <0457.g1:/ B H />`. :just:`[G.166] <G.166>`

.. subclause::

   But :g:`/ B A / <0457.g1:/ B A />` is :eq:`equal <0457.g1:/ B A / = 0457.g1:/ E H />` to :g:`/ E H / <0457.g1:/ E H />`;

.. assertion::

   therefore, as :g:`/ B E / <0457.g1:/ B E />` is to :g:`/ E H / <0457.g1:/ E H />`, so is :g:`/ E H / <0457.g1:/ E H />` to :g:`/ H B / <0457.g1:/ H B />`.

.. subclause::

   And :g:`/ B E / <0457.g1:/ B E />` is greater than :g:`/ E H / <0457.g1:/ E H />`;

.. assertion:: [G.151] <G.151>

   therefore :g:`/ E H / <0457.g1:/ E H />` is also greater than :g:`/ H B / <0457.g1:/ H B />`.

.. assertion::

   Therefore :g:`/ B E / <0457.g1:/ B E />` has been cut in extreme and mean ratio at :g:`H <0457.g1:H>`, and the greater segment :g:`/ H E / <0457.g1:/ H E />` is :eq:`equal <0457.g1:/ H E / = 0457.g1:/ A B / = 0457.g1:/ B C / = 0457.g1:/ C D / = 0457.g1:/ D E / = 0457.g1:/ E A />` to the side of the pentagon.

.. assertion::

   Similarly we can prove that :g:`/ A C / <0457.g1:/ A C />` has also been cut in extreme and mean ratio at :g:`H <0457.g1:H>`, and its greater segment :g:`/ C H / <0457.g1:/ C H />` is :eq:`equal <0457.g1:/ C H / = 0457.g1:/ A B / = 0457.g1:/ B C / = 0457.g1:/ C D / = 0457.g1:/ D E / = 0457.g1:/ E A />` to the side of the pentagon.

.. qed::

   :just:`Q. E. D.`
