Elements, Book three, Proposition thirty-five. <pause>
If two chords intersect inside a circle, the rectangle contained by the segments of one chord equals the rectangle contained by the segments of the other. <pause>
In circle A B C D, let chords A C and B D intersect at point E. We claim that the rectangle A E by E C equals the rectangle D E by E B. <pause>
If both chords pass through the center E, all four segments A E, E C, D E, and E B are equal radii, so the rectangles contained by them are immediately equal. <pause>

[bell:construction] [style:brisk]
Now suppose the chords do not pass through the center. Find the center F of circle A B C D, and draw perpendiculars F G to A C and F H to B D. <pause>
Join the radii F B and F C, and join segment F E from the center to the intersection of the chords. <pause>

[bell:insight] [style:brisk]
Because F G passes through the center perpendicular to chord A C, it bisects A C at G. Since A C is cut equally at G and unequally at E, Book two Proposition five shows that the rectangle A E by E C plus the square on E G equals the square on G C. <pause>
Adding the square on F G to both sides, the Pythagorean theorem on right triangles F G E and F G C turns those sums into the square on F E and the square on radius F C. By the exact same reasoning on chord B D, the rectangle D E by E B plus the square on F E equals the square on radius F B. <pause>
Since radii F C and F B have equal squares, subtracting the common square on F E leaves the rectangle A E by E C equal to the rectangle D E by E B, <pause> as was required to prove. <pause>

[bell:system] [style:brisk]
Euclid proves four cases depending on whether one or neither chord passes through the center, while Campanus condensed the proof into this single general case. Together with Proposition thirty-six, it establishes the interior Power of a Point theorem: for any point E inside a circle of radius R at distance d from the center, the product of the chord segments through E is always R squared minus d squared. <pause>
