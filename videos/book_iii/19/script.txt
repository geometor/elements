Book Three, Proposition Nineteen. If a straight line touch a circle, and from the point of contact a straight line be drawn at right angles to the tangent, the center of the circle will be on the straight line so drawn. <pause> Let the straight line D E touch the circle A B C at the point C, and from C let C A be drawn at right angles to D E. We claim that the center of the circle is on A C.

[bell:construction]
[style: methodical, precise constructive cadence]
For suppose it is not, but, if possible, let a point F outside A C be the center, and let C F be joined.

[bell:insight]
[style: luminous, deductive geometric clarity]
Since the straight line D E touches the circle A B C, and F C has been joined from the supposed center to the point of contact, by Three Eighteen F C is perpendicular to D E; therefore the angle F C E is right. <pause> But the angle A C E was also drawn right; therefore by Postulate Four angle F C E equals angle A C E—the part equal to the whole, which by Common Notion Five is impossible. <pause> Therefore F is not the center of circle A B C; and similarly neither is any other point except a point on A C. Therefore the center of the circle lies on A C. As was required to prove.

Proposition Three Nineteen completes the triad of circular tangency: the normal to a tangent at the point of contact necessarily passes through the circle's center.
