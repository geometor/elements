Book Three, Proposition Eleven. If two circles touch one another internally, and their centers be taken, the straight line joining their centers, if it be also produced, will fall on the point of contact of the circles. <pause> Let the two circles A B C and A D E touch one another internally at the point A, and let the center F of circle A B C, and the center G of circle A D E, be taken. We claim that the straight line joined from G to F and produced falls on A.

[bell:construction]
[style: methodical, precise constructive cadence]
For suppose it does not, but, if possible, let it fall outside as F G H, meeting the inner circle at D and the outer circle at H, and let the radii A F and A G be joined.

[bell:insight]
[style: luminous, deductive geometric clarity]
In triangle A F G, by One Twenty, any two sides are greater than the remaining side, so A G and G F together are greater than F A—that is, greater than the outer radius F H. Subtracting the common segment F G from each, the remainder A G is greater than the remainder G H. <pause> But A G and G D are radii of the inner circle A D E, so A G equals G D. Therefore G D is greater than G H—the part greater than the whole, which is impossible. <pause> Therefore, the line joined from F through G cannot fall outside the point of contact; it must fall on A. As was required to prove.
