Book Three, Proposition Twenty-Seven. In equal circles, angles standing on equal arcs are equal to one another, whether they stand at the centers or at the arcs. <pause> Let A B C and D E F be equal circles, and on equal arcs B C and E F let angles B G C and E H F stand at the centers G and H, and angles B A C and E D F stand at the arcs. We must prove that angle B G C equals E H F, and angle B A C equals E D F.

[bell:construction]
[style: methodical, precise constructive cadence]
For if angle B G C is unequal to angle E H F, one of them is greater. Let B G C be greater, and on segment B G at G, construct angle B G K equal to angle E H F by One Twenty-Three.

[bell:insight]
[style: luminous, deductive geometric clarity]
Because equal central angles stand on equal arcs by Three Twenty-Six, arc B K equals arc E F. <pause> But arc E F was given equal to arc B C; therefore arc B K equals arc B C, the less to the greater, which is impossible. Thus central angle B G C is not unequal to E H F, so B G C equals E H F. <pause> And by Three Twenty, the inscribed angle B A C is half of central angle B G C, and angle E D F is half of E H F. Therefore angle B A C also equals angle E D F, as was required to prove.
