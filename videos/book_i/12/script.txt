[bell:proposition] Euclid's Elements, Book One, Proposition Twelve. <pause> Construct a perpendicular to a given line from a given point not on it. <pause> Given line A B and point C not on it, we are to construct from C a segment perpendicular to line A B.

[bell:construction] First, take a point D on the other side of line A B, and by Postulate Three, construct circle C D, with center C and defining radius point D. <pause> Circle C D intersects line A B at two points, E and G. <pause> Next, by Proposition Ten, bisect segment E G at point H, so that G H equals H E. <pause> Finally, by Postulate One, construct segments C G, C H, and C E.

[bell:insight] Since point C is the center of circle C D, Definition Fifteen guarantees that radius C G equals radius C E. <pause> Now compare triangles C H G and C H E: side G H equals side E H, side H C is common to both, and base C G equals base C E—so by Proposition Eight, Side-Side-Side congruence, the adjacent angles C H G and E H C are equal. <pause> And by Definition Ten, when a segment standing on a line makes the adjacent angles equal, each angle is a right angle and the standing segment is perpendicular to the line. <pause> Therefore segment C H has been constructed perpendicular to line A B from point C not on it, <pause> as was required to do.
