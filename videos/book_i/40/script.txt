[bell:proposition]
[style:brisk]
Euclid's Elements, Book One, Proposition Forty. <pause>
Equal triangles on equal bases and on the same side lie in the same parallels. <pause>
Let triangles A B C and C D E be equal in area, standing on equal bases B C and C E on the same straight line B E and on the same side of it. <pause>
We prove that the segment A D joining their vertices is parallel to base B E.

[bell:construction]
[style:brisk]
Join point A to point D with segment A D. <pause>
Suppose segment A D is not parallel to segment B E. <pause>
Then by Proposition Thirty-One, through point A draw segment A F parallel to segment B E, meeting segment C D at point F, and join point F to point E.

[bell:insight]
[style:brisk]
Since triangles A B C and F C E stand on equal bases B C and C E and lie in the same parallels B E and A F, <pause> by Proposition Thirty-Eight, triangle A B C equals triangle F C E. <pause>
Yet by hypothesis, triangle A B C equals triangle D C E. <pause>
By Common Notion One, things equal to the same thing are equal to one another, <pause> so triangle D C E equals triangle F C E, the greater equal to the less, which is impossible. <pause>
Thus segment A F cannot be parallel to segment B E, nor can any other line through point A except segment A D. <pause>
Therefore segment A D is parallel to segment B E, <pause> as was required to prove.
