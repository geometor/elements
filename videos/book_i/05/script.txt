[bell:proposition] Euclid's Elements, Book One, Proposition Five. <pause> In an isosceles triangle, the angles at the base are equal, and if the equal sides are extended, the angles under the base are equal. <pause> Given isosceles triangle A B C with side A B equal to side A C, and sides A B and A C extended to D and E, we prove that angle A B C equals angle A C B, and angle C B D equals angle B C E.

[bell:construction] First, by Postulate Two, extend segment A B to B D, and extend segment A C to C E. <pause> Second, take a point F on B D, and by Proposition Three, cut off from the greater segment A E a segment A G equal to A F. <pause> Third, by Postulate One, construct segments F C and G B.

[bell:insight] Now compare triangles A F C and A G B: since F A equals G A, A C equals A B, and they share the common vertex angle F A G, Proposition Four proves that base F C equals base G B, angle A C F equals angle A B G, and angle A F C equals angle A G B. <pause> Next, since the whole A F equals the whole A G, and within them A B equals A C, subtracting these equals by Common Notion Three leaves remainder B F equal to C G. <pause> Comparing the lower triangles B F C and C G B: since B F equals C G, F C equals G B, and angle B F C equals angle C G B, Proposition Four proves that the angles under the base, F B C and G C B, are equal, and angle B C F equals angle C B G. <pause> Finally, since the whole angle A B G equals A C F, and within them angle C B G equals B C F, subtracting these equals by Common Notion Three leaves angle A B C equal to angle A C B. <pause> Therefore in isosceles triangle A B C, the angles at the base are equal, and the angles under the base are equal, <pause> as was required to prove.
