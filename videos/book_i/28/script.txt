Euclid's Elements, Book One, Proposition Twenty-Eight. <pause>
If a transversal cutting two lines makes the exterior angle equal to the opposite interior angle, or the interior angles on the same side supplementary, the lines are parallel. <pause>
Let segment E F fall on segments A B and C D at points G and H, making exterior angle E G B equal to opposite interior angle G H D, or interior angles B G H and G H D equal to two right angles. <pause>
We prove that segment A B is parallel to segment C D.

[bell:construction]
[style:brisk]
First, suppose exterior angle E G B equals interior angle G H D. <pause>
By Proposition Fifteen, vertical angles E G B and A G H are equal. <pause>
By Common Notion One, alternate interior angles A G H and G H D are equal, and therefore by Proposition Twenty-Seven, segment A B is parallel to segment C D.

[bell:insight]
[style:brisk]
Second, suppose interior angles B G H and G H D sum to two right angles. <pause>
By Proposition Thirteen, adjacent angles A G H and B G H also sum to two right angles. <pause>
Subtracting common angle B G H from each by Common Notion Three leaves alternate angles A G H and G H D equal. <pause>
Therefore by Proposition Twenty-Seven, segment A B is parallel to segment C D, <pause> as was required to prove.
