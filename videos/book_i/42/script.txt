[bell:proposition]
[style:brisk]
Euclid's Elements, Book One, Proposition Forty-Two. <pause>
Construct, in a given angle, a parallelogram equal in area to a given triangle. <pause>
Given triangle A B C and rectilineal angle D, we construct in angle D a parallelogram equal in area to triangle A B C.

[bell:construction]
[style:brisk]
Bisect base B C at point E so that segment B E equals segment E C, and construct segment A E. <pause>
On segment E C at point E, construct angle C E F equal to the given angle D. <pause>
Through point A construct segment A G parallel to E C, and through point C construct segment C G parallel to E F, forming parallelogram F E C G.

[bell:insight]
[style:brisk]
Since base B E equals base E C, triangles A B E and A E C stand on equal bases and in the same parallels B C and A G. <pause>
By Proposition Thirty-Eight, triangle A B E equals triangle A E C, and therefore the whole triangle A B C is double triangle A E C. <pause>
By Proposition Forty-One, parallelogram F E C G is also double triangle A E C, since they share base E C and lie in the same parallels E C and A G. <pause>
Therefore by Common Notion One, parallelogram F E C G equals triangle A B C, and has angle C E F equal to the given angle D, <pause> as was required to do.
