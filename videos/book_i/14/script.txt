[bell:proposition] [style:brisk] Euclid's Elements, Book One, Proposition Fourteen. <pause> If two segments at a point on a line on opposite sides make adjacent angles summing to two right angles, the two segments are collinear. <pause> Given segment A B and segments B C and B D at point B on opposite sides, making adjacent angles A B C and A B D equal to two right angles, we prove that B D is in a straight line with C B.

[bell:construction] [style:brisk] Suppose B D is not in a straight line with B C, and let B E be in a straight line with C B. <pause> Since segment A B stands on the straight line C B E, Proposition Thirteen guarantees that the adjacent angles A B C and A B E equal two right angles.

[bell:insight] [style:brisk] Because angles A B C and A B D also equal two right angles, and all right angles are equal by Postulate Four, the sum of angles C B A and A B E equals the sum of angles C B A and A B D. <pause> Subtracting the common angle C B A from each leaves angle A B E equal to angle A B D — the lesser equal to the greater, which is impossible. <pause> Therefore B E is not in a straight line with C B, and neither is any other segment except B D. Thus C B is in a straight line with B D, <pause> as was required to prove.
