[bell:proposition]
[style:brisk] Euclid's Elements, Book One, Proposition Twenty-Two. <pause> Construct a triangle from three segments equal to three given segments satisfying the triangle inequality. <pause> Given three segments A, B, and C, such that the sum of any two is greater than the remaining one, we construct a triangle whose sides equal A, B, and C.

[bell:construction]
[style:brisk] Set out line D E, terminated at point D and unlimited toward E. <pause> Cut off segment D F equal to A, segment F G equal to B, and segment G H equal to C. <pause> Next, construct circle F D with center F and defining radius point D, and construct circle G H with center G and defining radius point H. <pause> From point K where the two circles intersect, construct segments K F and K G.

[bell:insight]
[style:brisk] Because F is the center of circle F D, segment F K equals segment F D, which equals A. <pause> Likewise, because G is the center of circle G H, segment G K equals segment G H, which equals C. <pause> Since segment F G was made equal to B, the three sides K F, F G, and G K equal the three given segments A, B, and C. <pause> Therefore triangle K F G has been constructed from three segments equal to A, B, and C, <pause> as was required to do.
