[bell:proposition] [style:brisk] Euclid's Elements, Book One, Proposition Four. <pause> Triangles with two sides and the included angle equal are congruent. <pause> Given triangles A B C and D E F with segment A B equal to D E, <pause> segment A C equal to D F, and angle B A C equal to angle E D F, <pause> we prove that base B C equals base E F, triangle A B C equals triangle D E F, and the remaining angles are equal.

[bell:construction] [style:brisk] Apply triangle A B C to triangle D E F, placing point A on point D and segment A B on D E. Because A B equals D E, point B coincides with point E. <pause> <pause> And since angle B A C equals angle E D F, segment A C coincides with D F. Because A C equals D F, point C also coincides with point F. <pause> <pause> Now, because point B coincides with point E and point C coincides with point F, base B C coincides with base E F.

[bell:insight] [style:brisk] By Common Notion Four, things that coincide with one another are equal to one another. Thus base B C equals base E F, and triangle A B C equals triangle D E F. <pause> <pause> Furthermore, the remaining angles coincide and are equal: angle A B C equals angle D E F, and angle A C B equals angle D F E. <pause> <pause> Therefore base B C equals base E F, triangle A B C equals triangle D E F, and the remaining angles are equal, <pause> as was required to prove.
