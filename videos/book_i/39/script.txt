[bell:proposition]
[style:brisk]
Euclid's Elements, Book One, Proposition Thirty-Nine. <pause>
Equal triangles on the same base and on the same side lie in the same parallels. <pause>
Let triangles A B C and D B C be equal in area, standing on the same base B C and on the same side of it. <pause>
We prove that the segment A D joining their vertices is parallel to base B C.

[bell:construction]
[style:brisk]
Join point A to point D with segment A D. <pause>
Suppose segment A D is not parallel to segment B C. <pause>
Then by Proposition Thirty-One, through point A draw segment A E parallel to segment B C, meeting segment B D at point E, and join point E to point C.

[bell:insight]
[style:brisk]
Since triangles A B C and E B C stand on the same base B C and lie in the same parallels B C and A E, <pause> by Proposition Thirty-Seven, triangle A B C equals triangle E B C. <pause>
Yet by hypothesis, triangle A B C equals triangle D B C. <pause>
By Common Notion One, things equal to the same thing are equal to one another, <pause> so triangle D B C equals triangle E B C, the greater equal to the less, which is impossible. <pause>
Thus segment A E cannot be parallel to segment B C, nor can any other line through point A except segment A D. <pause>
Therefore segment A D is parallel to segment B C, <pause> as was required to prove.
