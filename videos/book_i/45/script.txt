[bell:proposition]
[style:brisk]
Euclid's Elements, Book One, Proposition Forty-Five. <pause>
Construct, in a given angle, a parallelogram equal in area to a given polygon. <pause>
Let A B C D be the given polygon and E the given rectilineal angle. <pause>
We construct a parallelogram equal in area to polygon A B C D in an angle equal to E.

[bell:construction]
[style:brisk]
Construct segment D B, dividing polygon A B C D into triangles A B D and D B C. <pause>
By Proposition Forty-Two, construct parallelogram F K H G equal in area to triangle A B D in angle H K F equal to angle E. <pause>
Then, by Proposition Forty-Four, apply to segment G H parallelogram G H M L equal in area to triangle D B C in angle G H M equal to angle E.

[bell:insight]
[style:brisk]
Since angle E equals both angle H K F and angle G H M, angle H K F equals angle G H M. <pause>
Add angle K H G to each; then angles F K H and K H G equal angles K H G and G H M. <pause>
By Proposition Twenty-Nine, interior angles F K H and K H G between parallels F K and G H sum to two right angles, so angles K H G and G H M also sum to two right angles; <pause>
thus by Proposition Fourteen, K H and H M form a single straight segment K M. <pause>
Again, since transversal H G cuts parallels K M and F G, alternate angles M H G and H G F are equal. <pause>
Add angle H G L to each; since interior angles M H G and H G L sum to two right angles, angles H G F and H G L also sum to two right angles, <pause>
so F G and G L form a single straight segment F L. <pause>
Because F K and M L are each equal and parallel to H G, they are equal and parallel to one another, and segments K M and F L joining their extremities are also equal and parallel, making K F L M a parallelogram. <pause>
Finally, since triangle A B D equals parallelogram F K H G and triangle D B C equals parallelogram G H M L, <pause>
the whole polygon A B C D equals the whole parallelogram K F L M in angle F K M equal to angle E, <pause>
as was required to do.
