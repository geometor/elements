[bell:proposition]
[style:brisk]
Euclid's Elements, Book One, Proposition Thirty-Six. <pause>
Parallelograms on equal bases and in the same parallels are equal in area. <pause>
Let parallelograms A B C D and E F G H stand on equal bases B C and F G and lie between the same parallels A H and B G. <pause>
We prove that parallelogram A B C D equals parallelogram E F G H.

[bell:construction]
[style:brisk]
Construct segments B E and C H joining points B and C to points E and H. <pause>
Since base B C equals base F G, and by Proposition Thirty-Four opposite side F G equals E H in parallelogram E F G H, Common Notion One gives segment B C equal to segment E H.

[bell:insight]
[style:brisk]
Segments B C and E H are both equal and parallel, and segments E B and H C join their extremities in the same directions. <pause>
By Proposition Thirty-Three, segments E B and H C are themselves equal and parallel, so figure E B C H is a parallelogram. <pause>
By Proposition Thirty-Five, parallelogram E B C H equals parallelogram A B C D, since they share the same base B C between the parallels B C and A H. <pause>
For the same reason, parallelogram E F G H equals the same parallelogram E B C H on the shared base E H. <pause>
Therefore by Common Notion One, parallelogram A B C D equals parallelogram E F G H, <pause> as was required to prove.
