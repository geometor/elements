Book Two, Proposition Eight. If a segment be cut at random, four times the rectangle contained by the whole and one of the segments together with the square on the remaining segment is equal to the square described on the whole and the aforesaid segment as on one segment. <pause> Let the segment A B be cut at random at the point C.

[bell:construction]
[style: methodical, precise constructive cadence]
Produce A B in a straight line to D, and cut off B D equal to C B by One Three. Describe the square A E F D on A D by One Forty-Six, draw the diagonal D E, and draw the double figure with parallels C H and B L to A E, and M N and O P to A D, by One Thirty-One.

[bell:insight]
[style: luminous, deductive geometric clarity]
Because C B equals B D, opposite sides in the parallelograms give G K equal to K N and Q R equal to R P. By One Thirty-Six and One Forty-Three, the four small corner squares D K, C K, G R, and R N are all equal to one another, and are quadruple of C K. <pause> In the same way, the four rectangles A G, M Q, Q L, and R F are all equal to one another, and are quadruple of A G. Together, these eight areas form the gnomon S T U, which is quadruple of the whole rectangle A K—that is, four times the rectangle contained by A B and B D. <pause> Add the inner square O H, which equals the square on A C, to both. The gnomon S T U and O H complete the whole square A E F D on A D. <pause> Since B D equals B C, four times the rectangle contained by A B and B C together with the square on A C equals the square described on A B and B C as on one segment. Which was to be proved.
