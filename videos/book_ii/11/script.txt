Book Two, Proposition Eleven. To cut a given segment so that the rectangle contained by the whole and one of the segments is equal to the square on the remaining segment. <pause> Let A B be the given segment to be cut at a point H so that the rectangle A B, B H equals the square on A H.

[bell:construction]
[style: methodical, precise constructive cadence]
On A B, describe the square A B D C by One Forty-Six. Bisect A C at E by One Ten, and join B E. <pause> Produce C A to F, make E F equal to B E by One Three, describe the square F H on A F by One Forty-Six, and draw G H through to K.

[bell:insight]
[style: luminous, deductive geometric clarity]
Since A C is bisected at E and F A is added to it, by Two Six the rectangle C F, F A together with the square on A E equals the square on E F, which equals the square on E B. By One Forty-Seven, the square on E B equals the squares on A B and A E; subtracting the square on A E from each leaves the rectangle F K equal to the square A B D C. <pause> Subtract the common rectangle A K from each; then the remaining square F H equals the remaining rectangle H D. <pause> Because A B equals B D, H D is the rectangle contained by A B and B H, and F H is the square on A H. Therefore, A B has been cut at H so that the rectangle contained by A B and B H equals the square on A H. Which was to be done.
