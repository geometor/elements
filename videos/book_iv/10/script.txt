Book Four, Proposition Ten. To construct an isosceles triangle having each of the angles at the base double of the remaining one. <pause> Let any straight line A B be set out; it is required to construct on A B an isosceles triangle A B D having each of the angles at the base B D double of the vertex angle at A.

[bell:construction]
[style: methodical, precise constructive cadence]
By Two Eleven, cut A B at C in golden section so that the rectangle contained by A B and B C equals the square on C A. Describe circle A B—shown as B D E—with center A and defining radius point B, and by Four One fit into the circle the chord B D equal to A C. <pause> Join A D and D C, and by Four Five circumscribe circle A C D about triangle A C D.

[bell:insight]
[style: luminous, deductive geometric clarity]
Because rectangle A B, B C equals the square on A C, and A C equals B D, rectangle A B, B C equals the square on B D. Therefore by Three Thirty-Seven, B D is tangent to circle A C D at D, and by Three Thirty-Two the tangent-chord angle B D C equals the alternate inscribed angle D A C. <pause> Add angle C D A to each: the whole angle B D A equals the sum of angles C D A and D A C, which by One Thirty-Two equals the exterior angle B C D. And because radii A D and A B are equal, by One Five base angles B D A and D B A are equal, making all three angles B D A, D B A, and B C D equal to one another. <pause> In triangle B C D, equal angles at B and C give D C equal to B D, and therefore equal to C A. Hence in isosceles triangle A C D, angle C D A equals angle D A C, so exterior angle B C D is double angle D A C. <pause> Therefore in isosceles triangle A B D, each of the base angles B D A and D B A is double the vertex angle D A B, as was required to do.
