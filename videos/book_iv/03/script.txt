Book Four, Proposition Three. About a given circle to circumscribe a triangle equiangular with a given triangle. <pause> Let A B C be the given circle, and D E F the given triangle; it is required to circumscribe about the circle A B C a triangle equiangular with the triangle D E F.

[bell:construction]
[style: methodical, precise constructive cadence]
Produce E F in both directions to the points G and H. Find the center K of the circle A B C by Three One, and draw the radius K B across at random. On K B at the point K, construct the angle B K A equal to the exterior angle D E G, and the angle B K C equal to the exterior angle D F H, by One Twenty-Three. <pause> Through the points A, B, and C, draw L A M, M B N, and N C L touching the circle A B C by the Porism to Three Sixteen.

[bell:insight]
[style: luminous, deductive geometric clarity]
Because L M, M N, and N L touch the circle at A, B, and C, and K A, K B, and K C are radii to the points of contact, by Three Eighteen the angles at A, B, and C are right angles. In the quadrilateral A M B K, which divides into two triangles, the four interior angles equal four right angles; since angles K A M and K B M are right, the remaining angles A K B and A M B equal two right angles. <pause> Yet by One Thirteen, the adjacent angles D E G and D E F also equal two right angles, and angle A K B equals angle D E G; subtracting equals, the remaining angle A M B equals angle D E F. Similarly, angle L N B equals angle D F E. <pause> Therefore, by One Thirty-Two, the third angle M L N equals angle E D F. Thus the triangle L M N is equiangular with the triangle D E F and is circumscribed about the circle A B C. Which was to be done.

[bell:system]
[style: reflective, scholarly, architectural synthesis]
Proposition Four Three is the projective dual of Four Two: instead of copying interior angles onto a tangent to locate chords, Euclid copies exterior supplementary angles at the center K so that the cyclic quadrilateral A M B K recovers the interior angles at the tangent intersections. As Heath notes, because each central angle at K is supplementary to an angle of triangle D E F, each is less than two right angles, guaranteeing that the three tangents meet. Peletier and Borelli observed that drawing all three pairs of parallel tangents produces eight triangles in all: two circumscribed about the circle, and six for which the circle is an escribed circle. <pause> In the GEOMETOR G-Index, node G one hundred fifty-one synthesizes twelve direct prerequisites across fifty-nine ancestor nodes, uniting center finding, tangent perpendicularity, and angle-sum duality.
