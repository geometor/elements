Book Four, Proposition Seven. About a given circle to circumscribe a square. <pause> Let A B C D be the given circle; it is required to circumscribe a square about the circle A B C D.

[bell:construction]
[style: methodical, precise constructive cadence]
Draw two diameters A C and B D of the circle A B C D at right angles to one another through the center E. <pause> Through the points A, B, C, and D draw F G, G H, H K, and K F touching the circle A B C D by the Porism to Three Sixteen.

[bell:insight]
[style: luminous, deductive geometric clarity]
Because F G touches the circle and radius E A is joined to the point of contact, by Three Eighteen the angles at A are right; for the same reason the angles at B, C, and D are also right. <pause> Since angles A E B and E B G are both right, by One Twenty-Eight and One Thirty the tangents G H and F K are parallel to diameter A C, while F G and H K are parallel to diameter B D. <pause> Thus the figures are parallelograms, so by One Thirty-Four each side equals the equal diameters A C and B D, making F G H K equilateral; and because opposite angles in parallelogram G B E A are equal, angle A G B and the angles at H, K, and F are all right. Therefore F G H K is a square circumscribed about the circle A B C D. Which was to be done.
