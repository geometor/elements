Book Four, Proposition Five. About a given triangle to circumscribe a circle. <pause> Let A B C be the given triangle; it is required to circumscribe a circle about the triangle A B C.

[bell:construction]
[style: methodical, precise constructive cadence]
Bisect the sides A B and A C at the points D and E by One Ten, and from D and E draw D F and E F at right angles to A B and A C. <pause> They will meet either within the triangle A B C, or on the line B C, or outside B C. Join F A, F B, and F C.

[bell:insight]
[style: luminous, deductive geometric clarity]
Because A D equals D B, and D F is common and at right angles, by Side-Angle-Side congruence the base A F equals F B. <pause> By the exact same reasoning on side A C, C F also equals A F, so the three segments F A, F B, and F C are all equal to one another. <pause> Therefore the circle described with center F and radius F A passes through A, B, and C, and is circumscribed about the triangle A B C—with angle B A C acute when F falls inside, right when F lies on B C, and obtuse when F falls outside. Which was to be done.
