Book Four, Proposition Nine. About a given square to circumscribe a circle. <pause> Let A B C D be the given square; it is required to circumscribe a circle about the square A B C D.

[bell:construction]
[style: methodical, precise constructive cadence]
Join the diagonals A C and B D, and let them cut one another at E.

[bell:insight]
[style: luminous, deductive geometric clarity]
Because D A equals A B, A C is common, and base D C equals base B C, by One Eight angle D A C equals angle B A C, so angle D A B is bisected by A C. Similarly, each of the angles A B C, B C D, and C D A is bisected by the diagonals A C and B D. <pause> Since angle D A B equals angle A B C, their halves—angles E A B and E B A—are also equal, so by One Six side E A equals side E B. By the same reasoning, all four segments E A, E B, E C, and E D are equal to one another. <pause> Therefore the circle E A, described with center E and defining radius point A, passes through B, C, and D, circumscribing the square A B C D, as was required to do.
