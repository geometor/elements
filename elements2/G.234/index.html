VII.33

Given as many numbers as we please, to find the least of those which have the same ratio with them.
given A, B, C be the given numbers, as many as we please
assert A, B, C are either prime to one another or not.
[G.221]assert If A, B, C are prime to one another, they are the least of those which have the same ratio with them.
[G.202]construct But, if not, let D the greatest common measure of A, B, C be taken, and, as many times as D measures the numbers A, B, C respectively, so many units let there be in the numbers E, F, G respectively.
[G.216]assert Therefore the numbers E, F, G measure the numbers A, B, C respectively according to the units in D.
[G.210]assert Therefore E, F, G measure A, B, C the same number of times; therefore E, F, G are in the same ratio with A, B, C.
assert I say next that they are the least that are in that ratio.
assert If E, F, G are not the least of those which have the same ratio with A, B, C, there will be numbers less than E, F, G which are in the same ratio with A, B, C.
assert Let them be H, K, L
assert Therefore H measures A the same number of times that the numbers K, L measure the numbers B, C respectively.
assert As many times as H measures A, so many units let there be in M
assert Therefore the numbers K, L also measure the numbers B, C respectively according to the units in M.
assert And, since H measures A according to the units in M,
[G.216]assert Therefore M also measures A according to the units in H.
assert For the same reason M also measures the numbers B, C according to the units in the numbers K, L respectively ;
assert Therefore M measures A, B, C.
assert Since H measures A according to the units in M,
[G.222]assert Therefore H by multiplying M has made A.
assert For the same reason also
assert E by multiplying D has made A.
assert Therefore the product of E, D is equal to the product of H, M.
[G.219]assert Therefore, as E is to H, so is M to D.
assert E is greater than H ;
assert Therefore M is also greater than D.
assert It measures A, B, C :
assert Which is impossible, for by hypothesis D is the greatest common measure of A, B, C.
assert Therefore there cannot be any numbers less than E, F, G which are in the same ratio with A, B, C.
conclude Therefore E, F, G are the least of those which have the same ratio with A, B, C.
