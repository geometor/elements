G.105 — Heath¶

III.26

In equal circles equal angles stand on equal circumferences, whether they stand at the centres or at the circumferences.

Let ( A B C ), ( D E F ) be equal circles, and in them let there be equal angles, namely at the centres the angles < B G C ), < E H F ), and at the circumferences the angles < B A C ), < E D F ); I say that the circumference ( B K C ) is equal to the circumference ( E L F ).

For let / B C /, / E F / be joined.

Now, since the circles ( A B C ), ( D E F ) are equal,

the radii are equal.

Thus the two straight lines / B G /, / G C / are equal to the two straight lines / E H /, / H F /;

and the angle at G is equal to the angle at H;
[G.11]therefore the base / B C / is equal to the base / E F /.

And, since the angle at A is equal to the angle at D,

[G.101]the segment ( B A C ) is similar to the segment ( E D F );

and they are upon equal straight lines.

But similar segments of circles on equal straight lines are equal to one another; [G.103]

therefore the segment ( B A C ) is equal to ( E D F ).

But the whole circle ( A B C ) is also equal to the whole circle ( D E F ); therefore the circumference ( B K C ) which remains is equal to the circumference ( E L F ).

Therefore etc. Q. E. D.
