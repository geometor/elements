G.524 solid angle

A:term:**solid angle** <solid-angle> is the inclination constituted by more than two:term:lines <G.2> which meet one another and are not in the same:term:surface <G.533>, towards all the lines. Otherwise: A:term:**solid angle** <solid-angle> is that which is contained by more than two:term:plane angles <G.22> which are not in the same:term:plane <G.7> and are constructed to one:term:point <G.1>.
definition solid-geometry book-xi solid-angle line surface plane-angle plane-surface point plane

G.525 solid angle contained

If a solid angle be contained by three plane angles, any two, taken together in any manner, are greater than the remaining one.
theorem proof solid-geometry book-xi point segment angle solid-angle plane-angle straight-line plane-surface plane equality

G.526 solid angle contained

Any solid angle is contained by plane angles less than four right angles.
theorem proof solid-geometry book-xi point segment angle triangle solid-angle plane-angle right-angle straight-line trilateral plane

G.528 construct a solid angle out of three plane

Construct a solid angle out of three plane angles two of which, taken together in any manner, are greater than the remaining one: thus the three angles must be less than four right angles.

If three segments be proportional, the parallelepipedal solid formed out of the three is equal to the parallelepipedal solid on the mean which is equilateral, but equiangular with the aforesaid solid.
