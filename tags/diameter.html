G.61 diameter of a circle

A diameter of a circle is any segment drawn through the center and terminated in both directions by the arc of the circle; it bisects the circle.
definition plane-geometry book-i diameter circle straight-line centre bisect arc

G.62 opposite sides, angles, and diagonal of a parallelogram

In a parallelogram, opposite sides and opposite angles are equal, and the diagonal bisects the area.
theorem proof plane-geometry book-i segment angle triangle quadrilateral plane-angle diameter parallel-straight-lines straight-line trilateral parallelogram bisect plane equality

G.66 triangles on the same base in the same parallels

Triangles on the same base and in the same parallels are equal in area.
theorem proof plane-geometry book-i point segment line triangle quadrilateral trilateral parallel-straight-lines figure diameter parallel equality

G.67 triangles on equal bases in the same parallels

Triangles on equal bases and in the same parallels are equal in area.
theorem proof plane-geometry book-i point segment line triangle quadrilateral trilateral parallel-straight-lines figure diameter parallel equality

G.70 parallelogram is double a triangle on the same base

If a parallelogram shares the same base with a triangle and lies in the same parallels, the parallelogram is double the triangle.
theorem proof plane-geometry book-i segment triangle quadrilateral trilateral parallel-straight-lines diameter parallel parallelogram equality

G.72 complements of parallelograms about the diagonal are equal

In any parallelogram, the complements of the parallelograms about the diagonal are equal in area.
theorem proof plane-geometry book-i segment triangle quadrilateral diameter trilateral parallelogram equality

G.86 gnomon

And in any parallelogrammic area let any one whatever of the parallelograms about its:term:diameter <G.61> with the two complements be called a:term:**gnomon** <gnomon>.
definition plane-geometry geometric-algebra book-ii diameter gnomon parallelogram

G.96 semicircle

A semicircle is the figure contained by a diameter and the arc cut off by it.
definition plane-geometry book-i semicircle figure diameter centre circle arc

G.105 diameter circle point

If on the diameter of a circle a point be taken which is not the center of the circle, and from the point segments fall upon the circle, that is greatest on which the center is, the remainder of the same diameter is least, and of the rest the nearer to the segment through the center is always greater than the more remote, and only two equal segments will fall from the point on the circle, one on each side of the least segment.
theorem proof plane-geometry circles book-iii point segment circle angle diameter centre straight-line trilateral plane-angle equality reductio-ad-absurdum

G.106 point taken outside

If a point be taken outside a circle and from the point segments be drawn through to the circle, one of which is through the center and the others are drawn at random, then, of the segments which fall on the concave arc, that through the center is greatest, while of the rest the nearer to that through the center is always greater than the more remote, but, of the segments falling on the convex arc, that between the point and the diameter is least, while of the rest the nearer to the least is always less than the more remote, and only two equal segments will fall on the circle from the point, one on each side of the least.
theorem proof plane-geometry circles book-iii point segment circle angle triangle straight-line centre diameter plane-angle trilateral arc equality reductio-ad-absurdum

G.116 segments circle diameter

Of segments in a circle the diameter is greatest, and of the rest the nearer to the center is always greater than the more remote.
theorem proof plane-geometry circles book-iii point segment circle angle straight-line diameter greater-distance-from-centre centre perpendicular plane-angle equality

G.118 segment drawn right

The segment drawn at right angles to the diameter of a circle from its extremity will fall outside the circle, and into the space between the segment and the arc another segment cannot be interposed; further the angle of the semicircle is greater, and the remaining angle less, than any acute angle.
theorem proof plane-geometry circles book-iii point segment circle angle triangle straight-line right-angle diameter extremity angle-of-segment plane-angle acute-angle rectilineal-angle centre trilateral tangent-to-circle semicircle arc plane equality reductio-ad-absurdum porism

G.139 thales semicircle theorem

In a circle the angle in the semicircle is right, that in a greater segment less than a right angle, and that in a less segment greater than a right angle; and further the angle of the greater segment is greater than a right angle, and the angle of the less segment less than a right angle.
