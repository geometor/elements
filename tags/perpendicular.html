G.33 right angle and perpendicular

When a segment standing on a line makes the adjacent angles equal, each of the equal angles is a right angle, and the standing segment is perpendicular to the line.

G.94 law of cosines for obtuse triangles

In obtuse-angled triangles, the square on the side subtending the obtuse angle is greater than the sum of the squares on the sides containing the obtuse angle by twice the rectangle contained by one of those sides and the projection of the other upon it.

G.95 law of cosines for acute triangles

If there be two equal plane angles, and on their vertices there be set up elevated segments containing equal angles with the original segments respectively, if on the elevated segments points be taken at random and perpendiculars be drawn from them to the planes in which the original angles are, and if from the points so arising in the planes segments be joined to the vertices of the original angles, they will contain, with the elevated segments, equal angles.
