Posts tagged 'right-angle'

G.18

When a straight line standing on a straight line makes the adjacent angles equal, each of the equal angles is a right angle, and the standing line is perpendicular to that on which it stands.
definition plane-geometry book-i right-angle perpendicular

G.19

Construct a line at right angles to a given line from a given point on it.
problem construction plane-geometry book-i point segment angle triangle right-angle equality

G.22

A ray standing on a line makes either two right angles or adjacent angles summing to two right angles.
theorem proof plane-geometry book-i point segment angle right-angle equality

G.23

All right angles are equal.
postulate plane-geometry book-i right-angle

G.24

If two rays at a point on a line on opposite sides make adjacent angles summing to two right angles, the two rays are collinear.
theorem proof plane-geometry book-i point segment angle right-angle equality reductio-ad-absurdum

G.28

In any triangle, the sum of any two angles is less than two right angles.
theorem proof plane-geometry book-i point segment angle triangle right-angle

G.40

If a transversal cutting two lines makes the exterior angle equal to the opposite interior angle, or interior angles on the same side supplementary, the lines are parallel.
theorem proof plane-geometry book-i segment angle right-angle parallel equality

G.41

Two lines intersected by a transversal with interior angles on one side summing to less than two right angles meet on that side.
postulate plane-geometry book-i right-angle

G.42

A transversal cutting parallel lines makes alternate interior angles equal, exterior and opposite interior angles equal, and same-side interior angles supplementary.
theorem proof plane-geometry book-i segment angle right-angle parallel equality

G.45

In any triangle, an exterior angle equals the sum of the two opposite interior angles, and the three interior angles sum to two right angles.
theorem proof plane-geometry book-i point segment line angle triangle right-angle equality

G.60

In a right-angled triangle, the square on the hypotenuse equals the sum of the squares on the two legs (Pythagorean Theorem).
theorem proof plane-geometry book-i point segment angle triangle quadrilateral polygon right-angle square equality

G.78

If in a circle a straight line through the centre bisect a straight line not through the centre, it also cuts it at right angles; and if it cut it at right angles, it also bisects it.
theorem proof plane-geometry circles book-iii point segment circle angle triangle right-angle bisect equality

G.94

The straight line drawn at right angles to the diameter of a circle from its extremity will fall outside the circle, and into the space between the straight line and the circumference another straight line cannot be interposed; further the angle of the semicircle is greater, and the remaining angle less, than any acute angle.
theorem proof plane-geometry circles book-iii point segment circle angle triangle right-angle semicircle arc equality reductio-ad-absurdum porism

G.97

If a straight line touch a circle, and from the point of contact a straight line be drawn at right angles to the tangent, the centre of the circle is on the straight line so drawn.
theorem proof plane-geometry circles book-iii point segment circle angle right-angle tangent equality reductio-ad-absurdum

G.100

The opposite angles of quadrilaterals in circles are equal to two right angles.
theorem proof plane-geometry circles book-iii segment circle angle triangle quadrilateral right-angle equality

G.110

In a circle the angle in the semicircle is right, that in a greater segment less than a right angle, and that in a less segment greater than a right angle; and further the angle of the greater segment is greater than a right angle, and the angle of the less segment less than a right angle.
theorem proof plane-geometry circles book-iii point segment circle angle triangle quadrilateral right-angle semicircle equality

G.123

Of quadrilateral figures, a square is equilateral and right-angled; an oblong is right-angled but not equilateral; a rhombus is equilateral but not right-angled; a rhomboid has opposite sides and angles equal but is neither equilateral nor right-angled.
definition plane-geometry book-i square oblong rhombus rhomboid trapezium equilateral right-angle

G.169

If two triangles have one angle equal to one angle, the sides about other angles proportional, and the remaining angles either both less or both not less than a right angle, the triangles is equiangular and will have those angles equal, the sides about which are proportional.
theorem proof plane-geometry proportions similar-figures book-vi point segment angle triangle right-angle proportion equality reductio-ad-absurdum

G.171

If in a right-angled triangle a perpendicular be drawn from the right angle to the base, the triangles adjoining the perpendicular are similar both to the whole and to one another.
theorem proof plane-geometry proportions similar-figures book-vi point segment angle triangle right-angle perpendicular similar equality porism

G.196

In right-angled triangles the figure on the side subtending the right angle is equal to the similar and similarly described figures on the sides containing the right angle.
theorem proof plane-geometry proportions similar-figures book-vi point segment angle triangle right-angle similar equality

G.446

A straight line is:term:**at right angles to a plane** <right-angles-to-plane>, when it makes right angles with all the straight lines which meet it and are in the plane.
definition solid-geometry book-xi right-angles-to-plane right-angle plane

G.447

If a straight line be set up at right angles to two straight lines which cut one another, at their common point of section, it is at right angles to the plane through them.
theorem proof solid-geometry book-xi point segment angle triangle right-angle plane equality

G.448

If a straight line be set up at right angles to three straight lines which meet one another, at their common point of section, the three straight lines are in one plane.
theorem proof solid-geometry book-xi point segment angle right-angle plane equality reductio-ad-absurdum

G.449

If two straight lines be at right angles to the same plane, the straight lines is parallel.
theorem proof solid-geometry book-xi point segment angle right-angle parallel plane equality

G.451

If two straight lines be parallel, and one of them be at right angles to any plane, the remaining one is at right angles to the same plane.
theorem proof solid-geometry book-xi point segment angle right-angle parallel plane equality

G.455

To set up a straight line at right angles to a given plane from a given point in it.
theorem proof solid-geometry book-xi point segment right-angle plane

G.456

From the same point two straight lines cannot be set up at right angles to the same plane on the same side.
theorem proof solid-geometry book-xi point segment angle right-angle plane equality reductio-ad-absurdum

G.458

Planes to which the same straight line is at right angles is parallel.
theorem proof solid-geometry book-xi point segment angle triangle polygon right-angle parallel plane reductio-ad-absurdum

G.462

A:term:**plane is at right angles to a plane** <plane-at-right-angles-to-plane> when the straight lines drawn, in one of the planes, at right angles to the common section of the planes are at right angles to the remaining plane.
definition solid-geometry book-xi plane-at-right-angles-to-plane right-angle plane

G.463

If a straight line be at right angles to any plane, all the planes through it is at right angles to the same plane.
theorem proof solid-geometry book-xi point segment angle polygon right-angle plane

G.464

If two planes which cut one another be at right angles to any plane, their common section is at right angles to the same plane.
theorem proof solid-geometry book-xi point segment polygon right-angle plane reductio-ad-absurdum

G.466

Any solid angle is contained by plane angles less than four right angles.
theorem proof solid-geometry book-xi point segment angle triangle right-angle plane solid-angle

G.468

Construct a solid angle out of three plane angles two of which, taken together in any manner, are greater than the remaining one: thus the three angles must be less than four right angles.
problem construction solid-geometry book-xi point segment circle angle triangle right-angle plane solid-angle equality reductio-ad-absurdum lemma
