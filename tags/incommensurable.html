G.372 commensurable and incommensurable magnitudes

Those magnitudes are said to be:term:**commensurable** <commensurable> which are:term:measured <G.253> by the same measure, and those:term:**incommensurable** <incommensurable> which cannot have any common measure.

G.373 less unequal magnitudes

If, when the less of two unequal magnitudes is continually subtracted in turn from the greater, that which is left never measures the one before it, the magnitudes is incommensurable.
theorem proof incommensurables magnitudes book-x point segment part-aliquot incommensurable reductio-ad-absurdum

G.378 incommensurable magnitudes ratio

Incommensurable magnitudes have not to one another the ratio which a number has to a number.
theorem proof incommensurables magnitudes book-x point incommensurable ratio number

G.379 magnitudes ratio number

If two magnitudes have not to one another the ratio which a number has to a number, the magnitudes is incommensurable.
theorem proof incommensurables magnitudes book-x point ratio number incommensurable

G.380 commensurable in square

Straight lines are:term:**commensurable in square** <commensurable-in-square> when the:term:squares <G.63> on them are:term:measured <G.253> by the same area, and:term:**incommensurable in square** <incommensurable-in-square> when the squares on them cannot possibly have any area as a common measure.
definition incommensurables magnitudes book-x straight-line commensurable-in-square square part-aliquot incommensurable-in-square commensurable incommensurable

G.381 squares segments commensurable

The squares on segments commensurable in length have to one another the ratio which a square number has to a square number; and squares which have to one another the ratio which a square number has to a square number will also have their sides commensurable in length. But the squares on segments incommensurable in length have not to one another the ratio which a square number has to a square number; and squares which have not to one another the ratio which a square number has to a square number will not have their sides commensurable in length either.

G.382 magnitudes proportional commensurable

If four magnitudes be proportional, and the first be commensurable with the second, the third is commensurable with the fourth; and, if the first be incommensurable with the second, the third is incommensurable with the fourth.
theorem proof incommensurables magnitudes book-x point proportional commensurable ratio number proportion incommensurable

G.385 magnitudes commensurable incommensurable

If two magnitudes be commensurable, and the one of them be incommensurable with any magnitude, the remaining one is incommensurable with the same.

G.388 incommensurable magnitudes added

If two incommensurable magnitudes be added together, the whole is incommensurable with each of them; and, if the whole be incommensurable with one of them, the original magnitudes is incommensurable.
theorem proof incommensurables magnitudes book-x point segment quadrilateral incommensurable part-aliquot straight-line square figure contained-rectangle equality reductio-ad-absurdum lemma

G.390 there unequal segments

If there be two unequal segments, and to the greater there be applied a parallelogram equal to the fourth part of the square on the less and deficient by a square figure, and if it divide it into parts which are incommensurable, the square on the greater is greater than the square on the less by the square on a segment incommensurable with the greater. And, if the square on the greater be greater than the square on the less by the square on a segment incommensurable with the greater, and if there be applied to the greater a parallelogram equal to the fourth part of the square on the less and deficient by a square figure, it divides it into parts which are incommensurable.

G.391 rational and irrational segments

With these hypotheses, it is proved that there exist:term:segments <G.4> infinite in multitude which are:term:commensurable <G.372> and incommensurable respectively, some in length only, and others in square also, with an assigned segment. Let then the assigned segment be called:term:**rational** <rational>, and those segments which are commensurable with it, whether in length and in square or in square only,:term:**rational** <rational>, but those which are incommensurable with it:term:**irrational** <irrational>.

G.392 rational and irrational areas

G.395 rectangle contained rational

The rectangle contained by rational segments commensurable in square only is irrational, and the side of the square equal to it is irrational. Let the latter be called medial.

The square on a medial segment, if applied to a rational segment, produces as breadth a segment rational and incommensurable in length with that to which it is applied.

G.410 rational segments commensurable

If two rational segments commensurable in square only be added together, the whole is irrational; and let it be called binomial.

G.413 segments incommensurable square

If two segments incommensurable in square which make the sum of the squares on them rational, but the rectangle contained by them medial, be added together, the whole segment is irrational: and let it be called major.

G.414 segments incommensurable square

If two segments incommensurable in square which make the sum of the squares on them medial, but the rectangle contained by them rational, be added together, the whole segment is irrational; and let it be called the side of a rational plus a medial area.

G.415 segments incommensurable square

If two segments incommensurable in square which make the sum of the squares on them medial, and the rectangle contained by them medial and also incommensurable with the sum of the squares on them, be added together, the whole segment is irrational; and let it be called the side of the sum of two medial areas.

G.458 segment there subtracted

G.459 segment there subtracted
