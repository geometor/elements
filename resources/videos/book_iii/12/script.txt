Book Three, Proposition Twelve. If two circles touch one another externally, the straight line joining their centers will pass through the point of contact. <pause> Let the two circles A B C and A D E touch one another externally at the point A, and let the center F of circle A B C, and the center G of circle A D E, be taken. We claim that the straight line joined from F to G passes through the point of contact at A.

[bell:construction]
[style: methodical, precise constructive cadence]
For suppose it does not, but, if possible, let it pass outside A as F C D G, meeting the two circles at C and D, and let the radii A F and A G be joined.

[bell:insight]
[style: luminous, deductive geometric clarity]
Because F is the center of circle A B C, the radius F A equals F C. And because G is the center of circle A D E, the radius G A equals G D. Therefore, the sum of F A and A G equals the sum of F C and G D. <pause> Since C and D lie along F G with the gap C D between them, the whole segment F G is greater than F C and G D together, and therefore greater than F A and A G together. Yet in triangle F A G, by One Twenty, the single side F G is also less than the two sides F A and A G together—which is impossible. <pause> Therefore, the straight line joined from F to G cannot fail to pass through the point of contact at A; it must pass through it. As was required to prove.
