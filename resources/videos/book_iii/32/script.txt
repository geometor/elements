Book Three, Proposition Thirty-Two. If a straight line touch a circle, and from the point of contact there be drawn across, in the circle, a straight line cutting the circle, the angles which it makes with the tangent will be equal to the angles in the alternate segments of the circle. <pause> Let the straight line E F touch the circle A B C D at the point B, and from B let the chord B D be drawn across cutting the circle.

[bell:construction]
[style: methodical, precise constructive cadence]
From B, draw B A at right angles to E F by One Eleven. Take a point C at random on the arc B D, and join A D, D C, and C B.

[bell:insight]
[style: luminous, deductive geometric clarity]
Because E F touches the circle at B and B A is drawn from the point of contact at right angles to the tangent, the center of the circle lies on B A by Three Nineteen. Therefore B A is a diameter of the circle, and by Three Thirty-One the angle A D B in the semicircle is a right angle. <pause> By One Thirty-Two, the three angles of triangle A B D equal two right angles, so the remaining two angles B A D and A B D equal one right angle. But the angle A B F is also right, so A B F equals the sum of B A D and A B D. Subtract the common angle A B D from each; the remaining angle D B F equals the angle B A D in the alternate segment of the circle. <pause> Again, because A B C D is a quadrilateral in a circle, its opposite angles B A D and B C D equal two right angles by Three Twenty-Two. The adjacent angles D B F and D B E on the tangent also equal two right angles by One Thirteen. Subtracting the equal angles D B F and B A D leaves the angle D B E equal to the angle D C B in the alternate segment D C B, as was required to prove.

[bell:system]
[style: reflective, scholarly, architectural synthesis]
Proposition Three Thirty-Two is the Alternate Segment Theorem: the angle between a tangent and a chord equals the inscribed angle subtended by that chord in the opposite arc. As Heath observes, the converse holds with equal elegance, and Pappus used this theorem to prove that any line through the point of contact of two tangent circles cuts off similar segments in each. <pause> In the GEOMETOR G-Index, node G one hundred forty synthesizes Three Nineteen, Three Twenty-Two, and Three Thirty-One into the engine that drives Euclid's circle-segment constructions in Three Thirty-Three and Three Thirty-Four, as well as the regular polygon inscriptions of Book Four.
