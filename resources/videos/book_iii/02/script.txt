Book Three, Proposition Two. If on the circumference of a circle two points be taken at random, the segment joining the points will fall within the circle. <pause> Let A B C be a circle, and let two points A and B be taken at random on its circumference.

[bell:construction]
[style: methodical, precise constructive cadence]
I say that the segment joined from A to B will fall within the circle. For suppose it does not, but, if possible, let it fall outside, as A E B. <pause> Take the center D of the circle A B C by Three One, join D A and D B, and draw D F E through to meet the circumference at F and the line at E.

[bell:insight]
[style: luminous, deductive geometric clarity]
Since the radii D A and D B are equal, by One Five the angles D A E and D B E are equal. And since one side A E B of triangle D A E is produced, by One Sixteen the exterior angle D E B is greater than angle D A E, and therefore greater than angle D B E. <pause> In triangle D B E, the greater angle is subtended by the greater side by One Nineteen, so D B is greater than D E. <pause> Yet D B equals the radius D F; therefore D F is greater than D E, the less than the greater—which is impossible. Neither will the segment fall on the circumference itself; therefore the chord A B falls within the circle. Which was to be proved.

Proposition Three Two establishes the strict convexity of the circle.
