Book Three, Proposition Thirty. To bisect a given arc. <pause> Let A D B be the given arc; it is required to bisect the arc A D B.

[bell:construction]
[style: methodical, precise constructive cadence]
Join the chord A B and bisect it at C by One Ten. <pause> From C, draw C D at right angles to A B by One Eleven, meeting the arc at D, and join A D and D B.

[bell:insight]
[style: luminous, deductive geometric clarity]
Because A C equals C B and C D is common, the two sides A C and C D equal the two sides B C and C D, and the included angle A C D equals the angle B C D, since each is right; therefore by One Four, the base A D equals the base D B. <pause> By Three Twenty-Eight, equal chords cut off equal arcs, the greater equal to the greater and the less to the less; and since each of the arcs A D and D B is less than a semicircle, the arc A D equals the arc D B. Therefore the given arc A D B has been bisected at D, as was required to do.
