Book Three, Proposition Twenty-Eight. In equal circles, equal segments cut off equal arcs, the greater equal to the greater and the less to the less. <pause> Let A B C and D E F be equal circles, and let equal chords A B and D E cut off A C B and D F E as greater arcs, and A G B and D H E as lesser arcs. We must prove that arc A C B equals arc D F E, and arc A G B equals arc D H E.

[bell:construction]
[style: methodical, precise constructive cadence]
Take the centers K and L of the two circles, and join the radii A K, K B, D L, and L E.

[bell:insight]
[style: luminous, deductive geometric clarity]
Because the circles are equal, their radii A K, K B, D L, and L E are all equal. And since the base A B equals the base D E, the three sides of triangle A K B equal the three sides of triangle D L E, so central angle A K B equals central angle D L E by One Eight. <pause> By Three Twenty-Six, equal angles at the centers stand on equal arcs, so the lesser arc A G B equals the lesser arc D H E. <pause> And since the whole circle A B C equals the whole circle D E F, subtracting the equal lesser arcs leaves the remaining greater arc A C B equal to the greater arc D F E, as was required to prove.
