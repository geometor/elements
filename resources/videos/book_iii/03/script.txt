Book Three, Proposition Three. If in a circle a segment through the center bisect a segment not through the center, it also cuts it at right angles; and if it cut it at right angles, it also bisects it. <pause> Let A B C be a circle, and in it let a diameter C D through the center meet a chord A B not through the center at F.

[bell:construction]
[style: methodical, precise constructive cadence]
Take the center E of the circle A B C, and join the radii E A and E B.

[bell:insight]
[style: luminous, deductive geometric clarity]
First, let C D bisect A B at F. Since A F equals F B, F E is common, and the radii E A and E B are equal, by One Eight the adjacent angles A F E and B F E are equal. Therefore each is a right angle by Definition Ten, and C D cuts A B at right angles. <pause> Conversely, let C D cut A B at right angles at F. Since the radii E A and E B are equal, by One Five the base angles E A F and E B F are equal. <pause> In triangles E A F and E B F, two angles equal two angles and the common side E F subtends equal angles; therefore by One Twenty-Six the remaining sides are equal, so A F equals F B. <pause> Thus a line through the center bisects a chord if and only if it is perpendicular to it. Which was to be proved.
