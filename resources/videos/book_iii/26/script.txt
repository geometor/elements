Book Three, Proposition Twenty-Six. In equal circles, equal angles stand on equal arcs, whether they stand at the centers or at the arcs. <pause> Let A B C and D E F be equal circles with centers G and H, having equal central angles B G C and E H F, and equal inscribed angles B A C and E D F. We must prove that arc B K C equals arc E L F.

Because the circles A B C and D E F are equal, their radii B G, G C, E H, and H F are all equal. And since the included central angle B G C equals E H F, triangles B G C and E H F are congruent by One Four, so base B C equals base E F. <pause> Furthermore, because the inscribed angle B A C equals E D F, the circular segments B A C and E D F are similar by Definition Eleven.
