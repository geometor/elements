“If on the diameter of a circle a point be taken which is not the center of the circle, and from the point segments fall upon the circle, that will be greatest on which the center is, the remainder of the same diameter will be least, and of the rest the nearer to the segment through the center is always greater than the more remote, and only two equal segments will fall from the point on the circle, one on each side of the least segment.”
— Euclid's Elements, BOOK III • PROP. 7 (G.105)
