Book Three, Proposition Fourteen. In a circle equal straight lines are equally distant from the center, and those which are equally distant from the center are equal to one another. <pause> Let A B D C be a circle, and let A B and C D be chords in it. We claim that A B and C D are equal if and only if they are equally distant from the center.

[bell:construction]
[style: methodical, precise constructive cadence]
Take the center E of circle A B D C by Three One. From E, draw E F and E G perpendicular to A B and C D, and join the radii A E and E C.

[bell:insight]
[style: luminous, deductive geometric clarity]
By Three Three, since E F through the center cuts the chord A B at right angles, it bisects it, so A B is double of A F; and for the same reason C D is double of C G. Thus A B equals C D if and only if the half-chords A F and C G are equal. <pause> Because the radii A E and E C are equal, their squares are equal. By One Forty-Seven, the Pythagorean theorem in the right triangles E F A and E G C, the squares on A F and E F equal the square on A E, and the squares on C G and E G equal the square on E C. Therefore, the sum of the squares on A F and E F equals the sum of the squares on C G and E G. <pause> When A B equals C D, subtracting the equal squares on A F and C G leaves the square on E F equal to the square on E G, so E F equals E G. Conversely, when E F equals E G, subtracting their equal squares leaves A F equal to C G, so the doubles A B and C D are equal. Therefore, in a circle equal chords are equally distant from the center, and chords equally distant from the center are equal. As was required to prove.
