Elements, Book three, Proposition thirty-seven. <pause>
From a point outside a circle, if a secant cuts the circle and another line falls on the circle such that the rectangle contained by the whole secant and its external segment equals the square on the line falling on the circle, then that line touches the circle. <pause>
From external point D, let secant D C A cut circle A B C, and let segment D B fall on the circumference at B so that the rectangle A D by C D equals the square on D B. We claim that D B is tangent to circle A B C. <pause>

[bell:construction] [style:brisk]
Using Book three Proposition seventeen, draw a tangent D E from D touching circle A B C at E. Find the center F of the circle, and join segments F E, F B, and F D. <pause>
Because D E is tangent to the circle at E, Book three Proposition eighteen shows that radius F E is perpendicular to D E, making angle F E D a right angle. <pause>

[bell:insight] [style:brisk]
By the tangent-secant theorem in Book three Proposition thirty-six, the rectangle A D by C D equals the square on tangent D E. Since the rectangle A D by C D also equals the square on D B by hypothesis, the square on D E equals the square on D B, and therefore segment D E equals segment D B. <pause>
In triangles F E D and F B D, radii F E and F B are equal, segments D E and D B are equal, and base F D is common. By Side-Side-Side congruence, angle D B F equals the right angle F E D. <pause>
Since D B stands at right angles to radius F B at its extremity B on the circle, the Porism to Book three Proposition sixteen proves that D B touches circle A B C, <pause> as was required to prove. <pause>
