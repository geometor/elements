Book Three, Proposition Thirty-One. In a circle, the angle in the semicircle is right, that in a greater segment is less than a right angle, and that in a less segment is greater than a right angle; and further, the angle of the greater segment is greater than a right angle, and the angle of the less segment is less than a right angle. <pause> Let A B C D be a circle with diameter B C and center E, and let chords B A, A C, A D, and D C be joined.

[bell:construction]
[style: methodical, precise constructive cadence]
Join the radius A E, and extend B A continuously in a straight line to F.

[bell:insight]
[style: luminous, deductive geometric clarity]
Because the radii B E and E A are equal, the base angles A B E and B A E are equal by One Five. Likewise, because C E equals E A, the angles A C E and C A E are equal. Therefore, the whole angle B A C equals the sum of the two angles A B C and A C B. <pause> By One Thirty-Two, the exterior angle F A C of triangle A B C also equals the sum of A B C and A C B. Thus angle B A C equals its adjacent angle F A C, so by Definition Ten each is a right angle. Therefore, the angle B A C in the semicircle is right. <pause> Next, by One Seventeen, the two angles B A C and A B C of triangle A B C are together less than two right angles; since B A C is right, angle A B C in the greater segment is less than a right angle. And by Three Twenty-Two, opposite angles A B C and A D C of the inscribed quadrilateral A B C D equal two right angles, so angle A D C in the less segment is greater than a right angle. <pause> Finally, the mixed angle of the greater segment—contained by arc A B C and chord A C—contains the right angle B A C and is greater than a right angle; while the angle of the less segment—contained by arc A D C and chord C A—is part of the right angle F A C and is less than a right angle, as was required to prove.

Proposition Three Thirty-One crowns the angle theory of Book Three with Thales' theorem: every angle inscribed in a semicircle is a right angle. As Heath notes from Aristotle's Posterior Analytics and Metaphysics, pre-Euclidean proofs took the midpoint of the semicircular arc and formed two isosceles right triangles, whereas Euclid's proof holds directly at any point on the arc by comparing the inscribed angle with its own exterior supplement. <pause> In the GEOMETOR G-Index, node G one hundred thirty-nine unites Book One's isosceles base angles and exterior angle theorem with Book Three's cyclic quadrilateral theorem, unlocking the tangent-chord constructions of the remainder of Book Three.
