Book Three, Proposition Five. If two circles cut one another, they will not have the same center. <pause> Let the circles A B C and C D G cut one another at the points B and C.

[bell:construction]
[style: methodical, precise constructive cadence]
I say that they will not have the same center. For, if possible, let their common center be E; join E C, and draw E F G through at random, meeting circle A B C at F and circle C D G at G.

[bell:insight]
[style: luminous, deductive geometric clarity]
Since the point E is the center of the circle A B C, the radius E C equals E F by Definition Fifteen. <pause> Again, since E is the center of the circle C D G, the radius E C equals E G. <pause> Because E C equals both E F and E G, E F is also equal to E G, the less equal to the greater—which is impossible. Therefore the point E is not the center of the circles A B C and C D G. Which was to be proved.
