Book Three, Proposition Twenty-Four. Similar segments of circles on equal segments are equal to one another. <pause> Let A E B and C F D be similar segments of circles standing on equal chords A B and C D. We are to prove that segment A E B equals segment C F D.

[bell:construction]
[style: methodical, precise constructive cadence]
Apply the segment A E B to C F D by placing point A on point C and the straight segment A B along C D. Because A B equals C D, point B also coincides with point D.

[bell:insight]
[style: luminous, deductive geometric clarity]
With base A B coinciding with base C D, if segment A E B does not coincide with C F D, it must either fall wholly within it, fall wholly outside it, or fall awry across it as C G D. <pause> It cannot fall wholly inside or outside, for by Three Twenty-Three two similar and unequal circle segments cannot stand on the same chord on the same side. Nor can it fall awry as C G D, for then two circles would intersect at more than two points—C, D, and their crossing point—which is impossible by Three Ten. <pause> Therefore segment A E B coincides everywhere with segment C F D, and by Common Notion Four, coinciding figures are equal; so segment A E B equals segment C F D, as was required to prove.
