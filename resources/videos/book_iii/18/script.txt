Book Three, Proposition Eighteen. If a straight line touch a circle, and a straight line be joined from the center to the point of contact, the straight line so joined will be perpendicular to the tangent. <pause> Let the straight line D E touch the circle A B C at the point C, let the center F of circle A B C be taken, and let F C be joined from F to C. We claim that F C is perpendicular to D E.

[bell:construction]
[style: methodical, precise constructive cadence]
For suppose it is not, and if possible, let F G be drawn from F perpendicular to D E, meeting the circle at B.

[bell:insight]
[style: luminous, deductive geometric clarity]
Then in triangle F C G, since angle F G C is right, by One Seventeen angle F C G is acute. Because the greater angle is subtended by the greater side, by One Nineteen F C is greater than F G. <pause> But F C equals F B as radii of circle A B C; therefore F B is also greater than F G—the part greater than the whole, which is impossible. <pause> Therefore F G is not perpendicular to D E; and similarly neither is any other straight line except F C. Therefore F C is perpendicular to the tangent D E. As was required to prove.
