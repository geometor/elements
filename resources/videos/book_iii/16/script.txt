Book Three, Proposition Sixteen. The straight line drawn at right angles to the diameter of a circle from its extremity will fall outside the circle, and into the space between the straight line and the circumference another straight line cannot be interposed; further, the angle of the semicircle is greater, and the remaining angle less, than any acute rectilineal angle. <pause> Let A B C be a circle about D as center and A B as diameter. We claim that the straight line drawn from A at right angles to A B falls outside the circle.

[bell:construction]
[style:brisk]
For suppose it does not, but, if possible, let it fall within as C A, and let the radius D C be joined.

[bell:insight]
[style:brisk]
Since D A equals D C, by One Five the base angle D A C equals the base angle A C D. But angle D A C is right, so angle A C D is also right; thus in triangle A C D, two angles equal two right angles, which by One Seventeen is impossible. Therefore the perpendicular at A cannot fall within the circle, nor on the circumference, so it falls outside as A E. <pause> Next, suppose another straight line F A could be interposed between A E and the arc C H A, and from D draw D G perpendicular to F A, meeting the circle at H. In right triangle A G D, the right angle A G D exceeds the acute angle D A G, so by One Nineteen the hypotenuse D A is greater than D G. Since D A equals the radius D H, D H would be greater than D G, the part greater than the whole, which is impossible. <pause> Therefore no straight line can be interposed between A E and the arc; the angle of the semicircle is greater, and the remaining horn-like angle less, than any acute rectilineal angle; and as the Porism states, the perpendicular at the extremity of a diameter touches the circle. As was required to prove.
