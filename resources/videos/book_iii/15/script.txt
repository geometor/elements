Book Three, Proposition Fifteen. Of straight lines in a circle the diameter is greatest, and of the rest the nearer to the center is always greater than the more remote. <pause> Let A B C D be a circle, let A D be its diameter and E the center, and let the chord B C be nearer to the center E, and F G more remote. We claim that the diameter A D is greatest, and that B C is greater than F G.

[bell:construction]
[style: methodical, precise constructive cadence]
From the center E, draw E H and E K perpendicular to B C and F G. Since B C is nearer to the center and F G more remote, by Definition Three Five, the perpendicular E K is greater than E H. <pause> On E K, cut off E L equal to E H by One Three. Through L, draw the chord M N at right angles to E K, and join the radii M E, E N, F E, and E G.

[bell:insight]
[style: luminous, deductive geometric clarity]
Because the perpendiculars E H and E L are equal, by Three Fourteen the chords B C and M N are also equal. Now the radii A E and E D equal M E and E N, so the diameter A D equals the sum of M E and E N. By One Twenty, in triangle M E N, the two sides M E and E N together are greater than M N—and since M N equals B C, the diameter A D is greater than B C. <pause> Furthermore, in triangles M E N and F E G, the two radii M E and E N equal the two radii F E and E G, while the central angle M E N is greater than the central angle F E G. By One Twenty-Four, the hinge theorem, the base M N is greater than the base F G; and since B C equals M N, B C is greater than F G. <pause> Therefore, the diameter A D is greatest, and the nearer chord B C is greater than the more remote chord F G. As was required to prove.
