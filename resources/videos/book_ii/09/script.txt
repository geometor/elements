Book Two, Proposition Nine. If a segment be cut into equal and unequal segments, the squares on the unequal segments of the whole are double of the square on the half and of the square on the segment between the points of section. <pause> Let the segment A B be cut into equal segments at C, and into unequal segments at D.

[bell:construction]
[style: methodical, precise constructive cadence]
From C, draw C E at right angles to A B by One Eleven, make C E equal to A C or C B by One Three, and join E A and E B. Through D, draw D F parallel to E C, and through F, draw F G parallel to A B by One Thirty-One, and join A F.

[bell:insight]
[style: luminous, deductive geometric clarity]
Because A C equals C E and the angle at C is right, the angles C A E and C E A are each half a right angle by One Five and One Thirty-Two. For the same reason, C E B and E B C are each half a right angle, so the whole angle A E B is right. By One Twenty-Nine and One Six, the small right triangles E G F and F D B are also isosceles, giving E G equal to G F, and F D equal to D B. <pause> By the Pythagorean theorem, One Forty-Seven, in right triangle A C E, the square on E A is double of the square on A C. And in right triangle E G F, the square on E F is double of the square on G F—which equals C D by One Thirty-Four. <pause> Therefore, the squares on A E and E F are double of the squares on A C and C D. Since angle A E F is right, the square on A F equals the squares on A E and E F; and since the angle at D is right, the squares on A D and D F also equal the square on A F. <pause> Because D F equals D B, the squares on A D and D B are double of the squares on A C and C D. Which was to be proved.
