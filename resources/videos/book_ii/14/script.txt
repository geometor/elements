Book Two, Proposition Fourteen. To construct a square equal to a given rectilineal figure. <pause> Let A be the given rectilineal figure; it is required to construct a square equal to A.

[bell:construction]
[style: methodical, precise constructive cadence]
By One Forty-Five, construct the rectangular parallelogram B D E C equal to the rectilineal figure A. If B E equals E D, B D E C is already a square; if not, let B E be greater, extend B E to F, and cut off E F equal to E D by One Three. <pause> Bisect B F at G by One Ten. With center G and radius G B or G F, describe the semicircle B H F; extend D E to meet the semicircle at H, and join G H.

[bell:insight]
[style: luminous, deductive geometric clarity]
Because the segment B F is cut into equal segments at G and unequal segments at E, by Two Five the rectangle contained by B E and E F together with the square on E G equals the square on G F, and therefore equals the square on radius G H. <pause> By the Pythagorean Theorem, One Forty-Seven, on right triangle G E H, the squares on E H and E G also equal the square on G H. Subtracting the common square on E G leaves the rectangle contained by B E and E F equal to the square on E H. <pause> Since E F equals E D, the rectangle contained by B E and E F is the rectangle B D E C, which equals A. Therefore, the square described on E H equals the given rectilineal figure A. Which was to be done.
