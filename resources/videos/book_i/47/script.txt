[style:brisk]
Euclid's Elements, Book One, Proposition Forty-Seven. <pause>
In a right-angled triangle, the square on the hypotenuse equals the sum of the squares on the two legs. <pause>
Let triangle A B C be a right-angled triangle having angle B A C right. <pause>
We prove that the square on segment B C equals the sum of the squares on segments B A and A C.

[bell:construction]
[style:brisk]
By Proposition Forty-Six, construct square B D E C on segment B C, square B A G F on segment B A, and square A C K H on segment A C. <pause>
By Proposition Thirty-One, through point A draw segment A L parallel to segments B D and C E, dividing the square on B C into rectangles B L and C L, and join segments A D, F C, A E, and B K.

[bell:insight]
[style:brisk]
Since angles B A C and B A G are both right angles, by Proposition Fourteen, segment C A is collinear with segment A G, and similarly segment B A is collinear with segment A H. <pause>
Right angles D B C and F B A are equal; adding common angle A B C makes angle D B A equal to angle F B C. <pause>
Because sides D B and B A equal sides B C and F B respectively, by Proposition Four, triangle A B D equals triangle F B C. <pause>
By Proposition Forty-One, rectangle B L is double triangle A B D on base B D between parallels B D and A L, while square G B is double triangle F B C on base F B between parallels F B and G C. <pause>
Therefore rectangle B L equals square G B, and by the same reasoning with triangles A C E and K C B, rectangle C L equals square H C. <pause>
Adding equals to equals, the whole square B D E C on segment B C equals the sum of squares G B and H C on segments B A and A C, <pause> as was required to prove.
