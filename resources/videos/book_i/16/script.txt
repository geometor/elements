[bell:proposition] [style:brisk] Euclid's Elements, Book One, Proposition Sixteen. <pause> In any triangle, if one side is extended, the exterior angle is greater than either opposite interior angle. <pause> Given triangle A B C with side B C extended to point D, we prove that exterior angle A C D is greater than either opposite interior angle B A C or C B A.

[bell:construction] [style:brisk] Using Proposition Ten, bisect segment A C at point E so that A E equals E C. <pause> Join segment B E and extend it to F, cutting off E F equal to B E by Proposition Three. <pause> Then join segment F C, and extend segment A C through to point G.

[bell:insight] [style:brisk] By Proposition Fifteen, vertical angles A E B and F E C are equal. <pause> Since sides A E and E B equal sides C E and E F, Proposition Four proves that triangles A B E and C F E are congruent, so angle B A E equals angle E C F. <pause> Because the whole exterior angle E C D is greater than its part E C F, exterior angle A C D is greater than angle B A C. <pause> Similarly, bisecting B C proves that vertical angle B C G, and therefore angle A C D, is also greater than angle C B A, <pause> as was required to prove.
