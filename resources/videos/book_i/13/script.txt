[bell:proposition] [style:brisk] Euclid's Elements, Book One, Proposition Thirteen. <pause> A segment standing on a line makes either two right angles or adjacent angles summing to two right angles. <pause> Given segment A B standing on segment C D at point B, making angles C B A and A B D, we prove that angles C B A and A B D are either two right angles or equal to two right angles.

[bell:construction] [style:brisk] First, if angle C B A equals angle A B D, then by Definition Ten they are already two right angles. <pause> Otherwise, from point B on C D, construct segment B E perpendicular to C D, so that angles C B E and E B D are two right angles.

[bell:insight] [style:brisk] Since angle C B E equals the sum of angles C B A and A B E, add angle E B D to each. Then the two right angles C B E and E B D equal the three angles C B A, A B E, and E B D. <pause> Again, since angle D B A equals the sum of angles D B E and E B A, add angle A B C to each. Then the adjacent angles D B A and A B C also equal the same three angles. <pause> Things equal to the same are equal to one another. Therefore the adjacent angles C B A and A B D equal the two right angles C B E and E B D, <pause> as was required to prove.
