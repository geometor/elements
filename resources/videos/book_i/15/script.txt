[bell:proposition] [style:brisk] Euclid's Elements, Book One, Proposition Fifteen. <pause> Intersecting lines make their vertical angles equal. <pause> Given segments A B and C D intersecting at point E, we prove that vertical angle A E C equals angle D E B, and angle C E B equals angle A E D.

[bell:construction] [style:brisk] Since segment A E stands on segment C D, Proposition Thirteen shows that the adjacent angles C E A and A E D equal two right angles. <pause> Again, since segment D E stands on segment A B, the adjacent angles A E D and D E B also equal two right angles.

[bell:insight] [style:brisk] Because all right angles are equal by Postulate Four, the sum of angles C E A and A E D equals the sum of angles A E D and D E B. <pause> Subtracting the common angle A E D from each leaves angle C E A equal to angle B E D. <pause> By the same reasoning, angle C E B equals angle D E A. Therefore intersecting lines make their vertical angles equal, <pause> as was required to prove.
