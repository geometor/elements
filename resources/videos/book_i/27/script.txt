[bell:proposition]
[style:brisk]
Euclid's Elements, Book One, Proposition Twenty-Seven. <pause>
If a transversal cutting two lines makes the alternate interior angles equal, the lines are parallel. <pause>
Let segment E F fall on segments A B and C D so that the alternate interior angles A E F and E F D are equal. <pause>
We prove that segment A B is parallel to segment C D.

[bell:construction]
[style:brisk]
Suppose segment A B is not parallel to segment C D. <pause>
Then when extended in both directions, they must meet either toward B and D, or toward A and C. <pause>
Let them be extended and meet toward B and D at point G, forming triangle G E F.

[bell:insight]
[style:brisk]
In triangle G E F, angle A E F is an exterior angle, and angle E F G is the opposite interior angle. <pause>
By hypothesis, angle A E F equals angle E F G. <pause>
Yet by Proposition Sixteen, the exterior angle of a triangle is strictly greater than either opposite interior angle, which is impossible. <pause>
Thus segments A B and C D cannot meet toward B and D, nor similarly toward A and C. <pause>
By Definition Twenty-Three, lines in the same plane that never meet in either direction are parallel. <pause>
Therefore segment A B is parallel to segment C D, <pause> as was required to prove.
