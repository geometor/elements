[bell:proposition]
[style:brisk] Euclid's Elements, Book One, Proposition Twenty-Four. <pause> If two triangles have two sides equal to two sides respectively, the one with the greater included angle has the greater base. <pause> Given triangles A B C and D E F with segment A B equal to D E, segment A C equal to D F, and angle B A C greater than angle E D F, we prove that base B C is greater than base E F.

[bell:construction]
[style:brisk] At point D on segment D E, construct angle E D G equal to the greater angle B A C. <pause> Make segment D G equal to A C and D F, and construct segments E G and F G.

[bell:insight]
[style:brisk] Because sides B A and A C equal sides E D and D G, and included angle B A C equals angle E D G, side-angle-side congruence gives base B C equal to base E G. <pause> Next, because D F equals D G, isosceles triangle D F G has angle D G F equal to angle D F G. <pause> Therefore angle D F G is greater than its part, angle E G F, and the whole angle E F G is much greater than angle E G F. <pause> In triangle E F G, the greater angle E F G is subtended by the greater side, so E G is greater than E F. <pause> Since B C equals E G, base B C is also greater than base E F, <pause> as was required to prove.
