[style:brisk]
Euclid's Elements, Book One, Proposition Thirty-Eight. <pause>
Triangles on equal bases and in the same parallels are equal in area. <pause>
Let triangles A B C and D E F stand on equal bases B C and E F and lie between the same parallel lines B F and A D. <pause>
We prove that triangle A B C is equal in area to triangle D E F.

[bell:construction]
[style:brisk]
Extend segment A D in both directions to points G and H. <pause>
By Proposition Thirty-One, through point B draw segment B G parallel to segment C A, and through point F draw segment F H parallel to segment D E.

[bell:insight]
[style:brisk]
Then each of the figures G B C A and D E F H is a parallelogram on equal bases B C and E F and between the same parallels B F and G H. <pause>
By Proposition Thirty-Six, parallelogram G B C A is equal in area to parallelogram D E F H. <pause>
By Proposition Thirty-Four, the diagonal A B bisects parallelogram G B C A, so triangle A B C is half of parallelogram G B C A. <pause>
Likewise, the diagonal D F bisects parallelogram D E F H, so triangle D E F is half of parallelogram D E F H. <pause>
Since the halves of equal things are equal to one another, triangle A B C is equal in area to triangle D E F, <pause> as was required to prove.
