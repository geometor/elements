[bell:proposition]
[style:brisk]
Euclid's Elements, Book One, Proposition Thirty. <pause>
Lines parallel to the same line are also parallel to one another. <pause>
Let each of the segments A B and C D be parallel to segment E F. <pause>
We prove that segment A B is also parallel to segment C D.

[bell:construction]
[style:brisk]
Let transversal segment G K fall upon segments A B, E F, and C D at points G, H, and K.

[bell:insight]
[style:brisk]
Since segment G K falls on parallel segments A B and E F, by Proposition Twenty-Nine, alternate interior angles A G K and G H F are equal. <pause>
Again, since segment G K falls on parallel segments E F and C D, by Proposition Twenty-Nine, exterior angle G H F equals opposite interior angle G K D. <pause>
By Common Notion One, things equal to the same thing are equal to one another, so angle A G K equals angle G K D. <pause>
Since these are alternate interior angles, by Proposition Twenty-Seven, segment A B is parallel to segment C D, <pause> as was required to prove.
