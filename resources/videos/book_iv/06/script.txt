Book Four, Proposition Six. In a given circle to inscribe a square. <pause> Let A B C D be the given circle; it is required to inscribe a square in the circle A B C D.

[bell:construction]
[style: methodical, precise constructive cadence]
Draw two diameters A C and B D of the circle A B C D at right angles to one another through the center E. <pause> Join the chords A B, B C, C D, and D A.

[bell:insight]
[style: luminous, deductive geometric clarity]
Because B E equals E D, since E is the center, and E A is common and at right angles, by Side-Angle-Side congruence the base A B equals the base A D. For the same reason, B C and C D are also equal to A B and A D, so the quadrilateral A B C D is equilateral. <pause> Next, because B D is a diameter, B A D is a semicircle; therefore by Three Thirty-One the angle B A D is right, and for the same reason angles A B C, B C D, and C D A are also right. <pause> Being both equilateral and right-angled, A B C D is a square by Definition Twenty-Two, and it has been inscribed in the given circle A B C D. Which was to be done.
