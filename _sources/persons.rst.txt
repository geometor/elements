======
People
======

.. rubric:: Biographical Register of Ancient Geometers, Commentators & Modern Editors

This unified biographical register documents the primary historical author, ancient commentators, critical editors, and modern formalizers of Euclid's *Elements*, alongside the philosophers, mathematicians, and scholars cited throughout the scholarly apparatus.

Authors, Translators & Editors
------------------------------

.. _person-euclid:

Euclid of Alexandria (fl. c. 300 BCE)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

*Alexandrian Geometer · Author of the Elements*

Compiled and systematized the Thirteen Books of the *Elements* at Alexandria under Ptolemy I Soter, integrating the foundational discoveries of the Pythagoreans, Hippocrates of Chios, Eudoxus of Cnidus, and Theaetetus of Athens into an axiomatic-deductive canon that served as the paradigm of mathematical rigor for two millennia.

.. _person-heath:

Sir Thomas Little Heath (1861 – 1940)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

*British Classical Scholar, Mathematician & Historian of Greek Mathematics*

Fellow of Trinity College, Cambridge. Produced the definitive English critical translation and mathematical commentary, *The Thirteen Books of Euclid's Elements* (1908; 2nd ed. rev. 1926), translating Heiberg's critical Greek text with exhaustive scholarly introductions, textual notes, and mathematical commentary.

.. _person-proclus:

Proclus Diadochus (412 – 485 CE)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

*Neoplatonic Philosopher & Commentator on Euclid's Elements*

Head of the Platonic Academy in Athens whose *Commentary on the First Book of Euclid's Elements* preserves the priceless Eudemian Summary of early Greek geometry alongside ancient critiques, alternative demonstrations, and lemmas from Geminus, Posidonius, Heron, Ptolemy, and Pappus.

.. _person-pappus:

Pappus of Alexandria (fl. c. 320 CE)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

*Alexandrian Geometer & Mathematical Commentator*

Author of the *Mathematical Collection* (*Synagoge*) and an essential commentary on Book X of the *Elements* (preserved in Arabic). Pappus systematized the Treasury of Analysis (*Analyomenos*) and documented essential lemmas, projective properties, and solid geometry foundations.

.. _person-heiberg:

Johan Ludvig Heiberg (1854 – 1928)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

*Danish Classical Philologist & Critical Editor of Greek Mathematical Texts*

Professor of Classical Philology at the University of Copenhagen. Edited the authoritative Teubner critical Greek edition of Euclid's *Elements* (*Euclidis Elementa*, Vols. I–V, 1883–1888), identifying Codex Vaticanus Graecus 190 (P) as the primary pre-Theonine archetype and restoring the uncorrupted ancient text.

.. _person-phi-architect:

phi ARCHITECT
~~~~~~~~~~~~~

*Founder & Architect of GEOMETOR*

Creator of the GEOMETOR platform and compiler of ``geometor.elements``. Architected the symbolic dependency directed acyclic graph (G-Index: `G.1`–`G.601`), formal pseudocode representation, and multi-track interactive digital codex editions unifying ancient geometry with modern computational formalization.

Apollonius of Perga (c. 240 – c. 190 BC)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

*The 'Great Geometer' of Antiquity*

Author of the monumental treatise on Conics (introducing the terms parabola, hyperbola, and ellipse from the Pythagorean application of areas) as well as works on Plane Loci, Tangencies, and Unordered Irrationals.

.. _person-archimedes:

Archimedes of Syracuse (c. 287 – 212 BC)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

*Greek Geometer, Physicist & Engineer*

Perfected Eudoxus's method of exhaustion and mechanical heuristic discovery to determine areas, volumes, and centers of gravity of curvilinear and solid figures.

.. _person-archytas:

Archytas of Tarentum (428 – 347 BC)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

*Pythagorean Mathematician, Statesman & Friend of Plato*

Solved the duplication of the cube via the intersection of a cylinder, a cone, and a torus in three dimensions, and proved key propositions in harmonic number theory (that no mean proportional exists in a superparticular ratio, VIII.8).
