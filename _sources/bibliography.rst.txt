============
Bibliography
============

.. rubric:: Primary Editions, Manuscript Archetypes & Scholarly Literature

This unified bibliography documents the primary Greek critical editions, historical translations, and classical treatises underpinning the GEOMETOR edition of Euclid's *Elements*.

Primary Editions & Critical Texts
---------------------------------

.. _cite-heiberg-1883:

Euclidis Elementa (Heiberg Critical Edition, 1883–1888)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Citation Key**: ``heiberg:1883``
* **Editor**: :person:`Johan Ludvig Heiberg <heiberg>`
* **Full Title**: *Euclidis Elementa*, 5 volumes (Leipzig: B. G. Teubner, 1883–1888), forming Volumes I–V of *Euclidis Opera Omnia*.
* **Significance**: The definitive modern critical Greek edition of the *Elements*, establishing the pre-Theonine text directly from *Codex Vaticanus Graecus 190*.

.. _cite-heath-1926:

The Thirteen Books of Euclid's Elements (Heath, 1908 / 1926)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Citation Key**: ``heath:1926``
* **Translator & Editor**: :person:`Sir Thomas Little Heath <heath>`
* **Full Title**: *The Thirteen Books of Euclid's Elements, Translated from the Text of Heiberg with Introduction and Commentary*, 3 volumes (Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1908; 2nd ed. rev., 1926; reprinted New York: Dover Publications, 1956).
* **Significance**: The standard English critical edition, containing extensive historical introductions, textual notes, and mathematical commentary across all 13 books.

.. _cite-simson-1756:

The Elements of Euclid (Simson, 1756 / 1762)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Citation Key**: ``simson:1756``
* **Editor & Translator**: Robert Simson
* **Full Title**: *The Elements of Euclid, viz. the First Six Books, together with the Eleventh and Twelfth* (Glasgow: Robert & Andrew Foulis, 1756 [Latin], 1762 [English]).
* **Significance**: Landmark eighteenth-century Scottish restoration of Euclid's demonstrations that shaped British geometry education for more than a century.

.. _cite-proclus-commentary:

Commentary on the First Book of Euclid's Elements (Proclus)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Citation Key**: ``proclus:in-primum-euclidis``
* **Author**: :person:`Proclus Diadochus <proclus>`
* **Standard Edition**: G. Friedlein, *Procli Diadochi in primum Euclidis Elementorum librum commentarii* (Leipzig: B. G. Teubner, 1873); English translation by Glenn R. Morrow (Princeton University Press, 1970).
* **Significance**: Indispensable late-antique commentary preserving early Greek mathematical history (the Eudemian Summary) and Hellenistic criticisms.

Archival Manuscript Witnesses
-----------------------------

* **Codex Vaticanus Graecus 190 (Codex P)**: Ninth-century manuscript preserved in the Vatican Library. Discovered in 1808 by François Peyrard, this codex preserves the primary non-Theonine text upon which Heiberg based his critical edition.
* **Codex Bodleianus D'Orville 301**: Written in 888 CE in Constantinople by Stephen the Clerk for Arethas of Caesarea, now held at the Bodleian Library, Oxford. One of the oldest dated Greek mathematical manuscripts.
* **Codex Parisinus Graecus 2466**: Tenth-century manuscript preserved in the Bibliothèque nationale de France.
* **Codex Bononiensis 18–19**: Eleventh-century manuscript preserved in the University Library of Bologna.
